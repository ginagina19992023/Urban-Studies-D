## One-paragraph summary (for the email to Johanna)

> My essay examines Fuxing Island (复兴岛), a small former shipyard island in Shanghai's Huangpu River that was kept as an undeveloped "strategic reserve" for roughly 15 years before being rebranded in December 2024 as a state-backed "Quantum City" innovation hub, and further activated in August 2025 through RED LAND — an immersive ACGN (anime/comics/games) event hosted in partnership with the social media platform Xiaohongshu. Drawing on the Gentrification unit's theoretical tools (Smith's rent gap, Davidson's new-build/state-led gentrification, Lees's critique of legitimising discourse), I analyze how a state-owned development consortium used a specific combination of symbolic language, landmark objects, and staged events (following Yanow's framework, as applied by Savini & Dembski to Amsterdam-Noord) to convert an idle industrial asset into a monetizable cultural brand. My central argument concerns **who captures the value this process creates**: visitors' own social-media "check-in" behaviour (what Cao terms "prosumption") functions as unpaid promotional labour generating "digital capital," but whereas in Western platform-gentrification cases this value flows diffusely to private landlords through rising rents, on Fuxing Island — where the state owns the land and organised the platform partnership — it is captured directly by the state through a quantifiable voucher scheme tied to 500+ local merchants. I use the island's near-empty condition as an analytical advantage: with almost no pre-existing residents, the value-creation machinery is unusually easy to observe in isolation. I compare this with Harvey's account of 1980s Baltimore, Savini & Dembski's Amsterdam-Noord, and Yrjänä's (2026) Helsinki — the last being especially instructive, since Helsinki has comparable public land ownership yet chooses to use it to de-risk private investment rather than to capture value publicly.

## Core research question

**How is a fifteen-year-old rent gap on an almost-empty industrial island closed through platform-mediated, state-led urban branding — and who captures the value that this process creates?**

## Thesis statement

Fuxing Island's transformation demonstrates a **platform-mediated form of state entrepreneurialism**: the state (via a joint-venture development company) manufactures a place-brand through a coordinated sequence of symbolic language, landmark objects, and staged events, while visitors' own social-media "prosumption" generates the digital visibility that constitutes the value. The case's central finding is structural: because the state controls both the land and the platform partnership, **it — rather than a private landlord or the platform company — is positioned to capture that value**, and does so through a concrete, state-designed voucher mechanism rather than diffusely through the price mechanism of a private rental market. This is a genuine divergence from the Western platform-gentrification literature, where value typically flows to private property owners. A secondary and deliberately modest observation — that residual users experienced a narrow, non-price reorganisation of daily access — is reported with explicit acknowledgement of its evidentiary limits, and does not carry the argument.

## Key theoretical framework (beyond course readings, from the 3+ new sources)

1. **Harvey (1989)** — urban entrepreneurialism, esp. strategy of competing via the "spatial division of consumption" (tourism, spectacle, cultural innovation) — theoretical starting point
2. **Wu (2018) / Feng, Zhang & Wu (2026)** — "state entrepreneurialism": state retains central decision-making but mobilizes market instruments (here: a state-owned joint venture, 6:4 capital ratio) rather than ceding control to private developers
3. **Savini & Dembski (2016)** — Yanow's language/objects/acts framework for how symbolic politics manufacture a "creative city," applied to Amsterdam-Noord; directly transposable to Fuxing Island's "Quantum City" branding
4. **Cao (Urban Geography, and IJURR 2025)** — **"prosumption" as a value-creation mechanism** (visitors' unpaid content labour generating "digital capital") — the essay's core analytical tool. Her secondary finding on "aesthetic distinction" is used only as a minor supporting lens.
5. **Yrjänä (2026)** — comparative case, and the source of the essay's central comparative question: Helsinki has comparable public land ownership and planning monopoly, yet uses the "subsidy gap" to de-risk *private* investment rather than to capture value publicly — making it the sharpest available contrast to Fuxing Island's state-captured model

## Evidence base (primary/grey literature)

- Official Shanghai/Yangpu government announcements: strategic-reserve status (2009–2024), "Quantum City" launch (Dec 2024), RED LAND event (Aug 2025), "Fuxing Island Music Festival" and voucher-redemption commercial scheme (May 2026)
- Government transportation-control notice + Dinghai Road Sub-district "island affairs council" (岛事会) minutes — evidence of residual users' (delivery workers, water-utility workers, small number of residents) access being converted into a permit system during the event
- Feng, Zhang & Wu (2026) and den Hartog (2021) — two peer-reviewed academic sources whose field research independently covers this exact site/district and corroborate the governance structure and (in den Hartog's case) worker exclusion from the waterfront due to "time poverty"

## Essay structure (draft)

1. **Introduction** — hook: an idle shipyard becomes a "Quantum City" in 18 months; question: how is the rent gap closed, and **who captures the value**? Explicit framing of the island's emptiness as a *diagnostic advantage* (the machinery is visible in isolation)
2. **Literature review** — rent gap → new-build/state-led gentrification → legitimising discourse **and who captures value** (Lees, Yrjänä's subsidy gap) → symbolic politics (Yanow/Savini & Dembski) → **prosumption as value creation** (Cao)
3. **Case study** — (a) how the brand was built (language/objects/acts, chronological); (b) **core mechanism**: prosumption → digital capital → state-designed voucher scheme → merchant revenue (the essay's central finding); (c) *secondary, explicitly limited* observation on residual users' access
4. **Discussion** — comparison organised around **value capture**: Baltimore (private capture), Amsterdam-Noord (developer capture + electoral contestation), Helsinki (public land, but chooses private de-risking) vs. Fuxing Island (state capture, no contestation)
5. **Conclusion** — contribution: platform-mediated value creation is a shared mechanism, but **who captures it is a structural variable** depending on land ownership and governance; public capture is not automatically more equitable — it depends on accountability
