Abstract摘要

This article reports on a study of the Waterloo public housing estate in Sydney, Australia. In 2015, the state government announced the inner-city estate would be redeveloped to accommodate some affordable/social dwellings (30%) but with the majority of new dwellings being private market housing (70%). Based on ethnographic research conducted with residents of Waterloo between 2010 and 2017, we analyze the Waterloo redevelopment as an example of emplaced displacement. We draw on the work of geographer Doreen Massey and legal scholar Sarah Keenan to understand place as more than physical space, allowing us to conceptualize displacement as something more than simply the movement of people from one physical place to another. We bring to the fore the subjective experience of place, as articulated by public housing tenants, demonstrating that although they remain physically in place, the threat of eviction posed by the redevelopment significantly alters tenants’ spatial, sociocultural, and temporal relationship to place (i.e., the spaces tenants carry with them). The concept of embodied displacement seeks to capture the spatiotemporal diversity of low-income public renters’ experiences of loss of place.

本文报告一项关于澳大利亚悉尼 Waterloo 公屋区的研究。2015 年，州政府宣布将对这个内城公屋区进行再开发，部分为可负担/社会住房（30%），但大多数新住宅为私人市场住房（70%）。基于 2010 至 2017 年间对 Waterloo 居民所做的民族志研究，我们把 Waterloo 再开发作为“在地失所”（emplaced displacement）的一个例子来分析。我们借助地理学家 Doreen Massey 和法学学者 Sarah Keenan 的研究，把地方理解为不只是物理空间，从而把失所概念化为不只是人从一个物理地点迁往另一个地点。我们突出公屋租户所表达的、对地方的主观经验，说明尽管他们在物理上仍留在原地，再开发带来的驱逐威胁已经显著改变了租户与地方在空间、社会文化和时间上的关系（即租户“随身携带的空间”）。“具身失所”（embodied displacement）这一概念，试图捕捉低收入公共租户失去地方这一经验在时空上的多样性。

This article reports on a study conducted in the neighborhood of Waterloo, a suburb 3 km south of the Central Business District (CBD) of Sydney, Australia. The New South Wales (NSW) state government holds large tracts of public housing and public land in and around Waterloo, and the area’s high-rise public housing buildings are a defining landmark. As in other so-called global cities, the NSW state government is in the middle of a decades-long process of “renewing” public housing estates—to use the government’s policy language (NSW FACS, 2018). The broader area that falls under the government’s development agenda is known as Redfern-Waterloo, and it has been associated with Aboriginal and low-income housing in media and other public discourse for over four decades (Rogers & Darcy, 2014; Shaw, 2000). In 2018, the Waterloo public housing estate was home to around 4,000 residents in 2,012 dwellings (NSW FACS, 2018), with a high proportion of elderly residents and tenants with English as a second language (Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2017). The NSW Minister for Social Housing announced in December 2015 (NSW FACS, 2015) that the estate would be redeveloped to accommodate 10,000 people, some in affordable and social dwellings (30%), but with the majority (70%) in private-market housing in keeping with the government’s controversial social mix redevelopment agenda (Darcy, 2010). One objective of the social mix agenda is aimed at breaking up local “public housing” culture, which housing authorities suggest produces negative social outcomes (Darcy, 2010).

本文报告一项在 Waterloo 街区开展的研究。Waterloo 是澳大利亚悉尼中央商务区（CBD）以南 3 公里的一个郊区。新南威尔士州（NSW）政府在 Waterloo 及其周边持有大片公共住房和公有土地，该区的高层公屋是一个标志性地标。和其他所谓的全球城市一样，NSW 州政府正处在一个持续数十年的公屋区“更新”进程之中——借用政府的政策用语（NSW FACS, 2018）。纳入政府开发议程的更大区域被称为 Redfern-Waterloo，四十多年来，它在媒体和其他公共话语中一直与原住民和低收入住房联系在一起（Rogers & Darcy, 2014; Shaw, 2000）。2018 年，Waterloo 公屋区有约 4000 名居民，住在 2012 套住宅中（NSW FACS, 2018），老年居民和以英语为第二语言的租户比例很高（Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2017）。NSW 社会住房部长于 2015 年 12 月宣布（NSW FACS, 2015），这个公屋区将被再开发以容纳 10000 人，其中部分住可负担和社会住房（30%），但多数（70%）住私人市场住房，这符合政府颇具争议的社会混合再开发议程（Darcy, 2010）。社会混合议程的目标之一，是打破本地的“公屋”文化，住房机构认为这种文化会产生负面社会后果（Darcy, 2010）。

The redevelopment of the Waterloo estate is different to other public housing redevelopment projects in Sydney for two key reasons. First, medium- and high-rise public housing estates are the exception in Sydney, and in Australia in general, with the majority of stock comprising detached-dwelling public housing estates located in the middle- and outer-ring suburbs of the city. The high value of the land in this prime global city location is mediating what is possible in the Waterloo area through the redevelopment, especially in terms of the significant housing density increases that are possible at the site. These housing density increases were, for the most part, not possible in the earlier middle- and outer-ring public housing redevelopments. Second, and related to the first point, because of the significant density increases that are possible in Waterloo, there exists potential for the government to redevelop the area in such a way that allows the existing public housing tenants to remain in or return to the estate throughout or following the redevelopment. The land economics of the middle- and outer-ring public housing estate redevelopments in Sydney over the last 15 years often involved the removal of some—at times, all—of the public housing tenants to allow for the introduction of new private housing stock within the economic limits of the land and property markets in these areas (Bijen & Piracha, 2017; Rogers & Darcy, 2014). In the Sydney context, the Waterloo redevelopment offers a somewhat unique case of tenants remaining in place despite being evicted from their homes. We analyze this as an example of emplaced displacement to contribute to the conceptual debate about displacement in urban redevelopment more broadly.

Waterloo 公屋区的再开发与悉尼其他公屋再开发项目不同，原因有二。第一，中高层公屋区在悉尼乃至整个澳大利亚都是例外，大部分公屋存量是位于城市中环和外环郊区的独立住宅公屋区。这一全球城市黄金地段的高地价，决定了 Waterloo 通过再开发能做成什么，尤其是该地块可能实现的大幅住房密度提升。在早先的中环和外环公屋再开发中，这样的密度提升大多不可能。第二，与第一点相关，由于 Waterloo 可能大幅提高密度，政府有可能以一种让现有公屋租户在再开发期间或之后留在或返回该公屋区的方式进行再开发。过去 15 年悉尼中环和外环公屋区再开发的土地经济学，常常涉及迁走部分——有时是全部——公屋租户，以便在这些地区土地和房产市场的经济限度内引入新的私人住房存量（Bijen & Piracha, 2017; Rogers & Darcy, 2014）。在悉尼语境下，Waterloo 再开发提供了一个颇为独特的案例：租户虽被逐出自己的家，却仍留在原地。我们把它作为在地失所的一个例子来分析，以推进城市再开发中更广泛的关于失所的概念讨论。

The Waterloo case, we argue, exposes a variety of displacement experiences including displacement that occurs in the absence of the physical relocation of people. Displacement is a concept that is often recruited to understand the dynamics and experience of urban change. Despite its relevance for understanding a range of urban experiences and phenomena, much (but not all) of the debate about displacement has focused on questions about how to quantify, measure, and account for the physical displacement of people (as summarized by Davidson, 2009). A spatial assumption—that displacement requires the movement of a body from one physical space to another—was historically implicit within the narratives around the idea of urban displacement. However, many scholars, such as Davidson (2009), Davidson and Lees (2005), Marcuse (1985), and others, have called this spatial assumption into question by focusing on the experience of displacement and urban change, and thereby render less significant the role that physical movement might play. Indeed, when we align the ontology of place within the concept of displacement with mathematical space and/or physical movement, we render less visible—and perhaps even silence—a whole raft of urban displacement experiences.

我们认为，Waterloo 案例揭示了多种失所经验，包括在没有人被物理迁移的情况下发生的失所。失所是一个常被用来理解城市变迁动态与经验的概念。尽管它对理解一系列城市经验和现象很重要，关于失所的大部分（但不是全部）讨论，集中在如何量化、测量和统计人的物理迁移上（Davidson, 2009 有总结）。一个空间假设——失所需要一个身体从一个物理空间移到另一个物理空间——历来隐含在关于城市失所的叙事中。然而，Davidson（2009）、Davidson 和 Lees（2005）、Marcuse（1985）等许多学者，通过关注失所与城市变迁的经验，对这个空间假设提出了质疑，从而淡化了物理移动可能起的作用。确实，当我们把失所概念中的地方本体论等同于数学空间和/或物理移动时，就会让一大批城市失所经验变得不那么可见，甚至被噤声。

We focus here on the experiences of residents of public housing threatened with emplaced displacement. Although they have not yet physically moved, and although their homes have not yet been demolished, the threat of eviction has already done significant damage to their relationship to place. Disruptions to the temporal, spatial, and sociocultural relationship between residents and their home/place are commonly articulated, as we demonstrate by drawing on ethnographic data collected from Waterloo.

我们在这里关注面临在地失所威胁的公屋居民的经验。尽管他们还没有搬走，他们的家也还没被拆除，驱逐的威胁已经严重破坏了他们与地方的关系。正如我们借助在 Waterloo 收集的民族志资料将展示的，居民与家/地方之间在时间、空间和社会文化上的关系被打乱，是居民普遍表达的经验。

1. Displacement, Embodiment, and Urban Renewal1. 失所、具身性与城市更新

Geographers have long conceived of place as something more than a physical space that is located at a specific point in calendar time. At their most basic, space and time can be divided into objective and subjective elements (Rogers, 2014). The physical, objective mathematical points that mark out the borders of a public housing estate are easy to find in policy documents (Rogers, 2014). We are also familiar with the mathematically informed seconds, minutes, days, and years of the Gregorian calendar, which allow us to keep track of a place in calendar time. Governments use both objective and subjective representations of time and space to situate public housing tenants (subjects) and public housing estate (objects) in relation to neighborhoods (places). The government’s subjective representations include, for example, the various government policy discourses that seek to construct certain social and material pathologies about a place, or the complex policy symbolisms (e.g., economic rationales and dysfunctional tenants) that define and codify the people and places that become the focus of urban redevelopment (Rogers, 2014). What is often missing from these policy statements are the tenants’ subjective markers of place: the subjective, lived experiences of public housing tenants within specific demarcations of objective space and time. Gieryn (2000) describes place as having three aspects: a geographical location, a material form, and a site that is invested with meaning and value (p. 465). Massey (1995) argued that places are actively produced and are therefore inextricably linked to the social context within which they come into being. These constructions of place are the product of subjective perceptions, narratives, emotions, interpretations, and imaginings (Gieryn, 2000, p. 465).

地理学家早就把地方理解为不只是位于日历时间某一点上的物理空间。最基本地说，空间和时间可以分为客观和主观两部分（Rogers, 2014）。标出公屋区边界的物理、客观的数学坐标，很容易在政策文件中找到（Rogers, 2014）。我们也熟悉以数学为依据的格里高利历的秒、分、日、年，它们让我们能在日历时间中定位一个地方。政府同时使用对时间和空间的客观与主观表述，把公屋租户（主体）和公屋区（客体）放到与街区（地方）的关系中。政府的主观表述包括，例如，各种试图为某个地方建构某些社会和物质病理的政策话语，或者界定并编码成为城市再开发对象的人和地方的复杂政策符号（如经济理由和“功能失调的租户”）（Rogers, 2014）。这些政策表述中常常缺少的，是租户对地方的主观标记：公屋租户在特定的客观时空界线之内的主观生活经验。Gieryn（2000）把地方描述为具有三个方面：一个地理位置、一种物质形态，以及一个被赋予意义和价值的场所（p. 465）。Massey（1995）认为，地方是被主动生产出来的，因此与它得以形成的社会语境密不可分。这些对地方的建构，是主观感知、叙事、情感、解释和想象的产物（Gieryn, 2000, p. 465）。

Through our empirical analysis below, we interrogate the conceptualization of place within theories of displacement by bringing to the fore the subjective experience of place as articulated by public housing tenants in their neighborhood. We are following the work of other scholars (such as Bourdieu, 1979; Malpas, 1999; Massey, 1995, among many others), who, for decades across geography and the social sciences, have explicitly understood place to mean something more than a mathematically defined physical container (Massey, 1993). Despite this understanding, the policies that guide urban redevelopment are underwritten by an ontology of space that (a) constructs the place as a mathematical demarcation of physical space (e.g., a regulatory space that is marked out by the landed boundaries of the public housing estate); and (b) is a construction of place that silences the subjective narratives that public tenants create to talk about their experiences of their home in this place. Under this notion of place, the eviction and physical relocation of tenants from one neighborhood to another is considered displacement, whereas eviction that results in remaining in or returning to the neighborhood may not be. The Waterloo case therefore highlights avenues for understanding displacement by better understanding the objective and subjective complexities of this place. It also lends support to earlier calls to conceptualize the place in displacement as more complex than merely a movement in physical space.

通过下面的经验分析，我们追问失所理论中对地方的概念化，方法是突出公屋租户在自己的街区中所表达的对地方的主观经验。我们追随其他学者（如 Bourdieu, 1979; Malpas, 1999; Massey, 1995 等许多人）的研究，几十年来他们在地理学和社会科学中明确把地方理解为不只是一个数学上界定的物理容器（Massey, 1993）。尽管有这样的理解，指导城市再开发的政策所依据的空间本体论：（a）把地方建构为对物理空间的数学划分（例如由公屋区地块边界划出的一个监管空间）；（b）是一种使公共租户讲述其在此地之家的主观叙事被噤声的地方建构。在这种地方观念下，把租户从一个街区驱逐并迁往另一个街区被视为失所，而被驱逐后留在或返回原街区则可能不被视为失所。因此，Waterloo 案例通过更好地理解这个地方客观和主观上的复杂性，为理解失所开辟了途径。它也支持了早先的呼吁：把失所中的地方概念化为比物理空间中的移动更复杂的东西。

Throughout this analysis, we follow scholars, including Elliott-Cooper, Hubbard, and Lees (2019), who argue that we need to adopt more nuanced approaches to understanding displacement that capture the full range of displacements that occur within urban gentrifications, rather than simply being tied to 20th-century narratives that limit gentrification to being understood in Marcuse’s terms (which were highly relevant to the 1980s context of New York City, but fail to capture the breadth of experiences in settler-colonial cities like Sydney in the 21st century) and that rely on physical displacement as evidence for displacement. Davidson (2009) prompts urban scholars to reconsider displacement as loss of place, and not simply as the process of physical relocation. Similarly, Hyra (2015) describes the experiences of residents who stay put in a gentrifying neighborhood by noting the ways in which residents experienced a loss of place through cultural and political displacement, despite not being physically displaced. In Hyra’s case, the residents experienced a financially mediated displacement that resulted from the influx of higher income newcomers to the neighborhood.

在整个分析中，我们追随包括 Elliott-Cooper、Hubbard 和 Lees（2019）在内的学者，他们主张需要采用更细致的方法来理解失所，以捕捉城市绅士化中发生的各种失所，而不是只拘泥于二十世纪的叙事——那些叙事把绅士化限定在 Marcuse 的框架中理解（这对 1980 年代纽约的语境高度相关，却无法捕捉二十一世纪悉尼这样的定居者殖民城市的经验广度），并以物理迁移作为失所的证据。Davidson（2009）促使城市学者重新把失所理解为失去地方，而不只是物理迁移的过程。同样，Hyra（2015）描述了留在一个正在绅士化的街区里的居民的经历，指出这些居民尽管没有在物理上被迁走，却通过文化和政治失所经历了失去地方。在 Hyra 的案例中，居民经历的是由高收入新来者涌入所造成的、以金钱为中介的失所。

We understand emplaced displacement, then, to be a form of displacement following Elliott-Cooper et al.’s description of this phenomenon as “a form of violence that removes the sense of belonging to a particular community or home-space” (2019, p. 12)—that is, it is the relationship to a place that is displaced, rather than an individual being physically removed. Emplaced displacement is not only spatial, but—following Brickell, Arrigoitia, and Vasudevan (2017)—it is an “affective, emotional and material rupture” (Cooper et al., 2019, p. 494)—a severing not only of physical dwelling in space but of connections and ties that link people across time to a space in specific ways.

那么，我们把在地失所理解为一种失所形式，依循 Elliott-Cooper 等人对这一现象的描述：“一种移除对某个特定社区或家园空间之归属感的暴力形式”（2019, p. 12）——也就是说，被移置的是与一个地方的关系，而不是个人被物理迁走。在地失所不只是空间上的；依循 Brickell、Arrigoitia 和 Vasudevan（2017），它是一种“情感的、情绪的和物质的断裂”（Cooper et al., 2019, p. 494）——不仅切断了在空间中的物理居住，也切断了以特定方式把人与一个空间在时间上连起来的联系和纽带。

(Keenan, 2017, n.p.) draws on critical race and feminist theorists to argue that governments create spaces as “seemingly politically neutral, uniform and fixed” as a necessary first step to securing their power over people and place. Keenan shows “that space in fact consists of multiple, dynamic realities; realities which affect different people in different ways.” Drawing on the work of geographer Doreen Massey, Keenan’s work usefully moves beyond thinking of places as bounded physical areas, because the same place “will be experienced and articulated differently by different people, this understanding accounts for the same place having mismatched and even contrasting meanings for different people” ((Keenan, 2017, n.p.).

Keenan（2017, n.p.）借助批判种族理论和女性主义理论家的研究指出，政府把空间建构为“看似政治中立、统一和固定的”，作为确保其对人和地方之权力的必要第一步。Keenan 表明，“空间实际上由多重、动态的现实构成；这些现实以不同方式影响不同的人。”借助地理学家 Doreen Massey 的研究，Keenan 的工作有益地超越了把地方看作有边界的物理区域的思路，因为同一个地方“会被不同的人以不同方式经验和表述，这种理解解释了为什么同一个地方对不同的人有着不相匹配、甚至截然相反的意义”（Keenan, 2017, n.p.）。

This Massey-inspired ontology of place, which is constituted by multiple and overlapping subjective and objective meanings, allows us to conceive of the government as acting beyond the physical boundaries of the public housing estate (Rogers, 2014). Keenan (2017., n.p.) further deploys the ideas of Anderson, Sharma, and Wright (2018) about the “borders [that] follow people and surround them as they try to access paid labour, welfare benefits, health, labour protections, education, civil associations and justice” to claim that one “way to understand the complicated connection between human subjects and the places we live in and move through is the idea of ‘taking space with you’”: that we take a particular space with us as we move. In this sense, urban renewal and displacement both ruptures and reinforces the spaces that public housing tenants take with them. Residential displacement—physical relocation from one part of the city to another—has received greater attention in the literature than have the spaces that public housing tenants take with them as they remain in place amid an influx of new, affluent residents (Hyra, 2015, p. 1755; Keenan, 2017.). But perhaps more importantly, the more foundational concern should be that urban renewal and displacement stands to rupture the spaces that public housing tenants take with them through redevelopment in all the wrong ways—that is, in ways that will further contain and marginalize them. A Massey-inspired ontology of place within displacement requires us to reorient our attention toward the experiences of those who are unable to integrate the space they take with them into the new social, cultural, and economic dynamics of the suburb that is coming into being through urban renewal. As Slater argues, by focusing “on the voices of those who live in public housing,” we bring forward “a compelling view from below that is wildly at odds with that from above” (Slater, 2013, p. 384).

这种受 Massey 启发的地方本体论，由多重、重叠的主观和客观意义构成，使我们能够把政府理解为在公屋区物理边界之外也在发挥作用（Rogers, 2014）。Keenan（2017, n.p.）进一步借用 Anderson、Sharma 和 Wright（2018）关于“跟随人、包围人的边界”的观点——人们在试图获得有偿劳动、福利、医疗、劳动保护、教育、公民团体和司法时都会遇到这些边界——提出，理解人类主体与我们居住和穿行的地方之间复杂联系的一种“方式，是‘带着空间走’（taking space with you）的观念”：我们在移动时会随身带着一个特定的空间。在这个意义上，城市更新和失所既断裂、也强化了公屋租户随身携带的空间。相比于公屋租户在新的富裕居民涌入时留在原地所携带的空间，居住失所——从城市一处被物理迁往另一处——在文献中受到了更多关注（Hyra, 2015, p. 1755; Keenan, 2017）。但也许更重要的是，更根本的关切应当是：城市更新和失所可能以完全错误的方式——也就是以进一步限制和边缘化他们的方式——断裂公屋租户在再开发中随身携带的空间。一种受 Massey 启发、用于失所研究的地方本体论，要求我们把注意力转向那些无法把随身携带的空间融入城市更新正在形成的郊区新的社会、文化和经济动态之中的人的经验。正如 Slater 所说，通过关注“住在公共住房里的人的声音”，我们呈现出“一种来自底层的、与来自上层的看法截然不同的有力视角”（Slater, 2013, p. 384）。

Our data expose people who are concerned about being displaced in their own homes and neighborhoods. We note that the term replacement has been used by Hamnett (2003) in his dismissal of the phenomenon of gentrification-induced displacement, where he uses replacement to argue that class upgrading can be accounted for by the disappearance of the working class from society, rather than its displacement from urban space. This use of replacement has not been adopted by other scholars, and we, rather than following Hamnett, adopt a different notion of replacement as emplaced displacement, following Australian scholars such as Hulse and Reynolds (2017), who describe replacement as a process of gentrification without physical relocation.

我们的资料揭示了一些人担心在自己的家和街区里失所。我们注意到，“替换”（replacement）一词曾被 Hamnett（2003）用来否定绅士化引发的失所这一现象，他用“替换”来论证阶级升级可以用工人阶级在社会中的消失来解释，而不是用他们从城市空间中被迁走来解释。Hamnett 的这种用法未被其他学者采纳；我们不追随 Hamnett，而是追随 Hulse 和 Reynolds（2017）等澳大利亚学者，采用一种不同的“替换”概念，即在地失所——他们把替换描述为没有物理迁移的绅士化过程。

Hulse and Reynolds (2017) describe a dynamic associated with buy-to-let housing markets in Australia, which involves low-income households staying in place and in poverty despite housing prices rising around them: this is the space that the resident takes with them. Hulse and Reynolds (2017) show that this does not follow the standard gentrification-induced-displacement script as previously theorized, but rather that the upheaval of residents’ lives is occurring with them in place. Thus, Hulse and Reynolds (2017) use the term replacement instead of displacement. In this analysis, we follow the work on displacement-without-relocation outlined herein and adopt Hulse and Reynolds’ concept of replacement to describe the rupturing of the space that the residents take with them as they stay put. Emplaced displacement, then, refers to the rupturing of the spaces that public housing tenants take with them as they remain in place during a redevelopment.

Hulse 和 Reynolds（2017）描述了与澳大利亚“买房出租”（buy-to-let）住房市场相关的一种动态：低收入家庭在周边房价上涨时仍留在原地、仍处于贫困——这就是居民随身携带的空间。Hulse 和 Reynolds（2017）表明，这并不遵循此前理论化的标准的“绅士化引发失所”剧本，而是居民生活的动荡在他们留在原地时发生。因此，Hulse 和 Reynolds（2017）用“替换”一词取代“失所”。在本分析中，我们追随上文概述的关于“无迁移的失所”的研究，采用 Hulse 和 Reynolds 的替换概念，来描述居民留在原地时其随身携带空间的断裂。那么，在地失所指的是公屋租户在再开发期间留在原地时，他们随身携带之空间的断裂。

2. The Waterloo Case Study2. Waterloo 个案研究

This analysis draws upon ethnographic studies conducted over a long timeline. The primary empirical data used below come from a study conducted over a 14-month period, from November 2016 to December 2017, by Laura Wynne. This ethnographic research primarily comprised participant observation of meetings and events of groups engaged in issues around the redevelopment project, including a resident action group focused on opposing the redevelopment, a neighborhood committee comprising tenant representatives and local service organizations brought together to advise on community engagement and consultation around the redevelopment, and a number of capacity-building programs run by local community development organizations. Data were collected in the form of fieldnotes that captured general mood and sentiment as well as specific quotes and researcher observations. These data, used in conjunction with Dallas Rogers’ long-term research interest in the Redfern/Waterloo area (Arthurson et al., 2014; Porter et al., 2013; Rogers, 2014, 2016b; Rogers, Darcy, & Arthurson, 2017), provide a rich picture of the neighborhood as it has undergone change. Together, we observed hundreds of hours of public meetings and forums run by both government departments and local community organizations between 2010 and 2017.

本分析依据的是跨越很长时间的民族志研究。下面使用的主要经验资料，来自 Laura Wynne 于 2016 年 11 月至 2017 年 12 月进行的为期 14 个月的研究。这项民族志研究主要包括对围绕再开发项目开展活动的群体的会议和活动进行参与观察，这些群体包括一个反对再开发的居民行动小组、一个由租户代表和本地服务组织组成、就再开发的社区参与和咨询提供建议的街区委员会，以及本地社区发展组织开办的一些能力建设项目。资料以田野笔记的形式收集，记录了总体情绪和氛围，以及具体引语和研究者的观察。这些资料，结合 Dallas Rogers 对 Redfern/Waterloo 地区的长期研究兴趣（Arthurson et al., 2014; Porter et al., 2013; Rogers, 2014, 2016b; Rogers, Darcy, & Arthurson, 2017），为这个经历变迁的街区提供了一幅丰富的图景。我们两人在 2010 至 2017 年间共同观察了由政府部门和本地社区组织举办的数百小时的公开会议和论坛。

The redevelopment of Waterloo is, at the time of writing, still in the planning stages. This planning stage has been ongoing since 2015, when the redevelopment was first announced. Although this means that we cannot report on residents’ reflections following the redevelopment, what we can report on here are residents’ fears and concerns regarding expectations of neighborhood change and upheaval that they feel will result from the redevelopment. The data show that the disruption of connection to place is already in effect, in the absence of any physical redevelopment commencing.

在本文写作时，Waterloo 的再开发仍处于规划阶段。这个规划阶段自 2015 年首次宣布再开发以来一直在持续。这意味着我们无法报告居民在再开发之后的反思，但我们能在这里报告的，是居民对他们认为再开发将带来的街区变化和动荡的担忧与顾虑。资料显示，在任何物理再开发开始之前，与地方联系的断裂就已经在发生了。

Waterloo has a long history of embodied displacement and upheaval. The area is home to the Aboriginal people of the Gadigal Clan of the Eora Nation. The Gadi clan, whose land stretched from Burrawurra (Sydney Harbor’s South Head) west to Blackwattle Creek, maintained the land through burnings and the maintenance of paths throughout their country. After the European invasion and the displacement of the Gadigal people from their land, Boxley’s Clear, as Redfern was known in the early 1800s, continued to have importance as a meeting point for dispersed members of the Gadigal people and of their neighboring clans. Eventually, however, European settlers became intolerant of their presence and the Gadigal were moved on to Waterloo, Alexandria, and beyond (Sydney Echo, 1890).

Waterloo 有着漫长的具身失所和动荡的历史。这里是 Eora 族 Gadigal 氏族原住民的家园。Gadi 氏族的土地从 Burrawurra（悉尼港南头）向西一直延伸到 Blackwattle Creek，他们通过焚烧和维护穿越其领地的小径来照料这片土地。欧洲入侵、Gadigal 人被逐出其土地之后，被称为 Boxley’s Clear 的地方（Redfern 在 1800 年代初的名称）仍然是离散的 Gadigal 人及其邻近氏族成员的重要聚集点。然而，欧洲定居者最终无法容忍他们的存在，Gadigal 人被驱赶到 Waterloo、Alexandria 乃至更远的地方（Sydney Echo, 1890）。

Aboriginal connection with Waterloo did not cease with European settlement, and an Aboriginal community—nowadays representing a diverse number of Aboriginal nations, not just the Eora Nation but others from around the region and the continent—continues to live and work in the area. Waterloo and the adjacent neighborhood of Redfern remain a center of major cultural and political significance to the Aboriginal community. The following quote, from Wiradjuri man Joel Sherwood-Spring and Wiradjuri/Gamilaroi woman Lorna Monro, points to the decolonial space young Aboriginal people take with them in the area; they want to “critique colonisation and gentrification showing there is a Blak History to your flat white” (Sherwood-Spring & Monro, 2018, n.p.).

原住民与 Waterloo 的联系并未随欧洲定居而中断，一个原住民社区——如今代表着众多原住民民族，不仅有 Eora 族，还有来自本地区和整个大陆的其他民族——继续在这里生活和工作。Waterloo 和毗邻的 Redfern 街区，至今仍是对原住民社区具有重大文化和政治意义的中心。下面这句话出自 Wiradjuri 族男子 Joel Sherwood-Spring 和 Wiradjuri/Gamilaroi 族女子 Lorna Monro，指向年轻原住民在该地区随身携带的去殖民空间；他们想要“批判殖民和绅士化，表明你那杯 flat white 背后有一段黑人历史”（Sherwood-Spring & Monro, 2018, n.p.）。

In the 1930s, governments were lobbied by “town planning activists and social reformers” to implement slum clearance measures (Allport, 1988, p. 106). The overcrowded, poorly maintained and inadequate housing of the inner city—mostly comprising Victorian-era terrace housing, often divided into multiple family units—was thought to be a threat to both moral and sanitary hygiene, and thus was determined to require demolition and rebuilding. Many residents were displaced farther west to suburban Sydney, and many were never given the opportunity to return to their former neighborhood (Allport, 1988).

1930 年代，“城镇规划活动人士和社会改革者”游说政府实施贫民窟清理措施（Allport, 1988, p. 106）。内城过度拥挤、维护不良、不合标准的住房——大多是维多利亚时代的联排住宅，常被分隔成多户合住——被认为对道德和卫生都构成威胁，因而被认定需要拆除重建。许多居民被迁往更西边的悉尼郊区，很多人再也没有机会回到原来的街区（Allport, 1988）。

The NSW state government continued redeveloping land around Redfern-Waterloo until the 1970s, by which time a large public housing estate comprising both low- and high-rise apartment buildings had been constructed. The demolition of working-class housing (predominantly comprising the Victorian-era terraces mentioned above) in the area and its replacement with modernist public housing was met with a great deal of community opposition, and these projects were ultimately brought to a halt by the introduction of a Green Ban by the NSW Builders and Laborers Federation, who used strikes to prevent work that involved the demolition of built heritage and of workers’ housing (Burgmann, 2000).

NSW 州政府在 Redfern-Waterloo 一带持续再开发土地直到 1970 年代，那时已经建成了一个由低层和高层公寓楼组成的大型公屋区。拆除该地区的工人阶级住房（主要是上述维多利亚时代联排住宅）并代之以现代主义公屋，遭到了社区的强烈反对，这些项目最终因 NSW 建筑工人联合会实施的“绿色禁令”而停止，该工会用罢工来阻止涉及拆除建筑遗产和工人住房的工程（Burgmann, 2000）。

It is these homes—2,200 public housing dwellings—that are now subject to a redevelopment project. The decision to redevelop the estate was in part connected to the decision to locate a new train station, as part of Sydney’s privatized metro line, adjacent to the site, thus creating an opportunity for transit-led “land value uplift” development (Hill PDA, 2016). The project is framed by the government as an “exciting” opportunity to build a “dynamic new community.” The government labels the project a renewal or revitalization project, and invokes an idea of the public interest to justify the redevelopment, arguing that value capture from the new private developments on site will allow a boost in affordable housing supply.

正是这些住宅——2200 套公屋——现在成了再开发项目的对象。再开发这个公屋区的决定，部分与在其旁边设立一个新车站（属于悉尼私有化地铁线的一部分）的决定有关，从而创造了一个以交通为导向的“地价提升”（land value uplift）开发机会（Hill PDA, 2016）。政府把这个项目描绘为建设一个“充满活力的新社区”的“令人兴奋的”机会。政府把它称为更新或复兴项目，并援引公共利益来为再开发辩护，声称从现场新建私人开发中获取的价值将用来增加可负担住房供给。

The redevelopment will see all existing public housing demolished, with privately developed residential and commercial buildings developed on the estate. Current state government documents claim that the residential buildings will have 30% of housing units allocated as social and affordable housing (to be managed by nongovernment landlords) and 70% privately owned dwellings (NSW Communities Plus Program, 2018). Residents have been promised that they will not be forced to leave the estate in the long term and that all who wish to be will eventually be rehoused on site, although some may need to be temporarily relocated throughout the development process (Pawson and Pinnegar [2018, pp. 323–326] discuss a similar process in the Bonnyrigg redevelopment). We now move into the analysis via three sections: spatial displacements, temporal displacements, and sociocultural displacements.

再开发将拆除所有现有公屋，在公屋区上开发私人住宅和商业建筑。目前的州政府文件称，住宅楼中 30% 的单元将分配为社会和可负担住房（由非政府房东管理），70% 为私人所有住宅（NSW Communities Plus Program, 2018）。居民被承诺，长期而言他们不会被迫离开公屋区，所有愿意留下的人最终都将在原址重新安置，尽管有些人在开发过程中可能需要暂时搬迁（Pawson 和 Pinnegar [2018, pp. 323–326] 讨论了 Bonnyrigg 再开发中的类似过程）。下面我们分三节进入分析：空间失所、时间失所和社会文化失所。

3. Spatial Displacements3. 空间失所

In this section we interrogate how objective and subjective notions of space are recruited and deployed by different actors in Waterloo. As we noted earlier, place is more than physical space; however, physical space remains a significant spatial context to place (e.g., the physical scale of the redevelopment that is set by the state government in their policy and redevelopment texts). This seemingly coherent, objective scale is mapped out by government agencies via the spatial boundaries of the public housing estate, but it is not always the most appropriate scale or spatial modality for understanding the space tenants take with them through displacement. The NSW Department of Families and Community Services is responsible for delivering the redevelopment, and has been key to setting this spatial context. Their Communities Plus program policy texts, for example, state that “everyone who currently lives in Waterloo can return to Waterloo” (NSW FACS, 2016), providing a reassurance that attempts to fix the spatial context of displacement to the physical boundaries of the estate and thereby dispel the idea that residents will be displaced as a result of the redevelopment.

本节追问不同行动者在 Waterloo 如何调用和运用客观与主观的空间观念。如前所述，地方不只是物理空间；但物理空间仍然是地方的一个重要空间语境（例如州政府在其政策和再开发文本中设定的再开发物理规模）。政府机构通过公屋区的空间边界划定了这个看似连贯、客观的尺度，但它并不总是理解租户在失所中随身携带之空间的最合适尺度或空间方式。NSW 家庭与社区服务部负责实施再开发，是设定这一空间语境的关键。例如，其 Communities Plus 计划的政策文本声明“目前住在 Waterloo 的每个人都可以回到 Waterloo”（NSW FACS, 2016），这种保证试图把失所的空间语境固定在公屋区的物理边界上，从而打消居民会因再开发而失所的想法。

Following these types of policy framings by governments, much scholarly work on displacement has focused on the neighborhood scale. This is partly due to the practicalities of collecting quantitative data about urban change on scales smaller than the neighborhood. For example, it is far more difficult to collect data regarding people being moved within a neighborhood than it is to document movements between neighborhoods. However, this focus has contributed to an understanding of displacement as a neighborhood-scale phenomenon, explaining why the government—and indeed others, too—might not conceive of the Waterloo case as displacement. Marcuse usefully defined displacement as “when any household is forced to move from its residence” (1985, p. 205), under a range of specific conditions, thus focusing on the residence rather than the neighborhood. Lewicka (2011) show that people often report stronger place attachments at the level of their body, home, and immediate neighborhood than at the level of the suburb, city, or nation state (Rogers, 2014). But attachments to place or a sense of home are multiscalar and can be hard to pin to a single scalar definition. Place attachments flow from the body through to the building and out into the neighborhood and beyond. The physical policy demarcation of the estate, therefore, is not a useful construct for understanding the spaces tenants take with them through redevelopment. Work by scholars such as Hubbard and Lees (2018) demonstrates the ways in which physical relocation can be distinguished from the loss of home: they describe the experiences of tenants subject to renewal processes in the UK who, although compensated for the loss of property through compulsory purchase orders, are not adequately compensated for the loss of home, which residents see as distinct from property.

在政府这类政策框架之后，许多关于失所的学术研究也聚焦于街区尺度。这部分是由于在小于街区的尺度上收集城市变迁的定量数据有实际困难。例如，收集人们在一个街区内部被迁移的数据，比记录在街区之间的迁移要困难得多。然而，这种聚焦助长了把失所理解为街区尺度现象的看法，这也解释了为什么政府——以及其他人——可能不把 Waterloo 案例看作失所。Marcuse 有益地把失所定义为“任何住户被迫搬离其住所之时”（1985, p. 205），附带一系列具体条件，因而聚焦于住所而非街区。Lewicka（2011）表明，人们常常报告说对自己的身体、家和紧邻街区的地方依恋，强于对郊区、城市或民族国家的依恋（Rogers, 2014）。但对地方的依恋或家的感觉是多尺度的，很难固定在单一的尺度定义上。地方依恋从身体流向建筑，再流向街区及更远处。因此，公屋区在政策上的物理划界，并不是理解租户在再开发中随身携带之空间的有用建构。Hubbard 和 Lees（2018）等学者的研究表明了物理迁移可以与失去家园区分开来的方式：他们描述了英国经历更新过程的租户的经历，这些租户虽然通过强制收购令获得了财产损失的补偿，却没有为失去家园得到足够补偿，而居民认为家园与财产是不同的。

Some residents of Waterloo reported that the prospect of moving within their neighborhood— from their existing apartment or building to a new or different one—was felt by them to be a form of displacement. When told that tenants “will not be made homeless” by the redevelopment, Catriona, a long-term resident, responded “yes, but we don’t know where it [home] is going to be” (tenant action group meeting, January 2017). For Catriona, a move within her neighborhood would be experienced as loss of home, despite not being made homeless. When a government representative told residents that “no one will need to leave the estate,” another resident responded, “yes, but the area will be completely different” (Corinne, tenant action group meeting, September 2017). Thus, the tenants’ concerns about displacement are related to the radical transformation of the neighborhood within the boundaries of the estate: despite not being physically displaced, the spatiality of their estate is being replaced.

一些 Waterloo 居民表示，在自己的街区内搬家——从现有的公寓或楼栋搬到一套新的或不同的公寓——在他们感受中也是一种失所。当被告知租户“不会因再开发而无家可归”时，长期居民 Catriona 回答：“是的，但我们不知道它（家）会在哪里”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 1 月）。对 Catriona 来说，在自己街区内搬家，会被体验为失去家园，尽管她没有变得无家可归。当一位政府代表告诉居民“没有人需要离开公屋区”时，另一位居民回答：“是的，但这个地方会完全不一样了”（Corinne，租户行动小组会议，2017 年 9 月）。因此，租户对失所的担忧，与公屋区边界之内街区的剧烈改变有关：尽管没有在物理上被迁走，他们公屋区的空间性正在被替换。

The building or block scale is rarely discussed in explorations of displacement; however, it was raised frequently in discussions with residents of Waterloo. One resident, Robert, felt that “the moment the first building is knocked down, this community will be nonexistent anymore” (tenant action group meeting, June 2017). Such statements highlight that attachments to place might be felt on a smaller scale than the neighborhood scale, and that the loss of a particular building (i.e., the spatiality of that building) might trigger experiences of displacement in residents.

在关于失所的探讨中，很少讨论楼栋或街区块的尺度；然而在与 Waterloo 居民的讨论中，这个尺度经常被提起。居民 Robert 觉得“第一栋楼被推倒的那一刻，这个社区就不复存在了”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 6 月）。这样的说法表明，对地方的依恋可能在比街区更小的尺度上被感受到，失去一栋特定的楼（即那栋楼的空间性）就可能在居民中引发失所的经验。

The neighborhood is defined differently by various actors, each of whom practices a different relation to the space. For government, this is largely about land ownership and zoning, whereas for tenants of the public housing estate, their relation to place is primarily oriented around their everyday practice of inhabiting the space. A disagreement about these understandings, which occurred in a community consultation meeting, sheds some light on the implications of such different understandings.

不同的行动者对街区有不同的界定，各自与这个空间形成不同的关系。对政府而言，这主要关乎土地所有权和分区；而对公屋租户而言，他们与地方的关系主要围绕居住在这个空间里的日常实践展开。在一次社区咨询会上发生的一场关于这些理解的分歧，揭示了这种不同理解的含义。

In this instance, a government representative was speaking to residents about information-sharing relating to the development of the new Metro Station on a block adjacent to the public housing estate. Despite this development being a key catalyst for the redevelopment project, the state government tends to treat them as largely different projects, because of key differences in landownership (private vs. public), stakeholders (businesses vs. public tenants), and government agency responsibilities relating to the two sites. As such, government representatives did not deem the public tenants to be directly affected by the redevelopment, because this was seen to be a separate pocket of the neighborhood, justified largely on the basis of boundaries drawn around publicly and privately owned lands. Several residents objected to this claim. One resident, Hannah, noted that tenants “don’t see the Metro and the redevelopment as separate projects” (Waterloo redevelopment group meeting, August 2017). Another noted that “people walk past [the Metro Station site] every day, they want to know what’s going on” (Waterloo redevelopment group meeting, August 2017). For residents, the metro site sits within the boundaries of their lived experience of the neighborhood—for many, it is in view of their home, or was passed on the way to the shops or the bus routes that service the area. In their experience, “the Metro and the redevelopment are not separate; this is just the beginning of a massive demolition” (Hannah, Waterloo redevelopment group meeting, August 2017).

当时，一位政府代表正在向居民介绍与公屋区毗邻地块上新地铁站开发有关的信息共享。尽管这项开发是再开发项目的关键催化剂，州政府往往把两者当作基本不同的项目，因为两个地块在土地所有权（私有 vs 公有）、利益相关方（企业 vs 公共租户）和政府机构职责上存在关键差别。因此，政府代表不认为公共租户直接受到这项开发的影响，因为那被看作街区的一个独立部分，理由主要是公有和私有土地之间划出的边界。好几位居民反对这种说法。居民 Hannah 指出，租户“并不把地铁和再开发看作两个独立的项目”（Waterloo 再开发小组会议，2017 年 8 月）。另一位指出，“人们每天都从（地铁站工地）旁边走过，他们想知道发生了什么”（Waterloo 再开发小组会议，2017 年 8 月）。对居民来说，地铁站工地就在他们所经验的街区边界之内——对许多人而言，它就在家门口的视野里，或是去商店、去搭公交路上经过的地方。在他们的经验中，“地铁和再开发不是分开的；这只是一场大规模拆除的开始”（Hannah，Waterloo 再开发小组会议，2017 年 8 月）。

This dispute serves as a reminder that boundaries of spatial areas such as a neighborhood are differentially defined depending on a range of experiences and knowledges. State government understands the site as distinct from the estate redevelopment, caused in large part by the way that urban planners differentiate between sites based on ownership and land-use (the Metro Station site sits on former commercial, rather than residential, lands). The state government uses technical means—land ownership patterns and zoning—to understand space, place, and the neighborhood. For residents, on the other hand, upheaval at the Metro Station site is contiguous with that occurring in their own immediate surroundings, because it is defined as a part of the space they understand to be their neighborhood.

这场争论提醒我们，街区这类空间区域的边界，会因不同的经验和知识而被不同地界定。州政府把这块地理解为与公屋区再开发不同的地块，这在很大程度上源于城市规划者按所有权和土地用途区分地块的方式（地铁站地块位于原来的商业用地而非住宅用地上）。州政府用技术手段——土地所有权格局和分区——来理解空间、地方和街区。而对居民来说，地铁站工地上的动荡与他们身边正在发生的变化是连成一片的，因为它属于他们所理解的自己街区的一部分。

This experience of displacement appears to be related not merely to the loss of the building in which one’s own home is situated, but also to a certain symbolism—an emotional attachment to place (Lewicka, 2011). The estate is currently distinct from surrounding areas because of its modernist urban form and béton brut design: tall, raw concrete buildings with large green expanses between. The built form of the Waterloo estate will undergo substantial alteration as buildings are demolished and new structures are built. This transformation in terms of architectural built form is not inconsequential for the experience of incumbent residents, whose attachment to this place is at least in part linked to the built form of the estate and its symbolic significance as a place designed specifically to shelter those in society most in need. Catriona notes that “Matavai and Turanga [towers] are models of their kind, they embody a vision for society; we might lose their legacy [if they are demolished]” (tenant action group meeting, January 2017). The redevelopment will transform the urban design of the area too, likely removing many of the public open spaces that serve local residents and demolishing their buildings. The symbolism of these buildings, purpose-built in a time of more government support for the poor and disadvantaged in the interest of social citizenship, is important for residents, many of whom feel connected not just to the buildings but to what they represent (i.e., these buildings hold space too).

这种失所经验似乎不仅与失去自己家所在的那栋楼有关，也与某种象征意义——对地方的情感依恋（Lewicka, 2011）——有关。这个公屋区目前因其现代主义城市形态和粗野主义（béton brut）设计而与周边地区不同：高耸的清水混凝土建筑，楼与楼之间是大片绿地。随着建筑被拆除、新建筑落成，Waterloo 公屋区的建成形态将发生重大改变。这种建筑形态上的转变，对现有居民的经验并非无关紧要，因为他们对这个地方的依恋至少部分与公屋区的建成形态，以及它作为一个专为社会最需要帮助的人提供庇护而设计的地方的象征意义相关。Catriona 指出，“Matavai 和 Turanga（塔楼）是同类建筑的典范，它们体现了一种社会愿景；（如果它们被拆除）我们可能会失去它们的遗产”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 1 月）。再开发还将改变该地区的城市设计，很可能移除许多服务本地居民的公共开敞空间，并拆除他们的楼。这些楼建于政府为了社会公民身份而更多支持穷人和弱势群体的时代，其象征意义对居民很重要，许多居民不仅与这些楼有联系，也与它们所代表的东西有联系（即这些楼本身也承载着空间）。

Residents’ responses to the wholesale transformation of the estate point to an experience of loss of place despite staying put. The area within the boundaries of the estate is expected to house around 10,000 people when the redevelopment is finalized—a significant increase on the current population of roughly 4,400 people. Enormous spatial upheaval will be required to transform the site, likely rendering the area unrecognizable as the spatial form is dramatically altered to fit the extra buildings. Residents are concerned that the neighborhood will not be able to accommodate the higher density: “there’s already little room enough, they’re going to squeeze thousands more in, there won’t be room for anything” (Eileen, tenant action group meeting, June 2017). Residents are concerned that they will not be able to integrate the space they take with them into this new high-density space; they fear that the rearrangements of space will not facilitate the reforging of their connections to place, and that they will find themselves out of place in the newly developed space, despite remaining physically in place.

居民对公屋区整体改造的反应，指向一种虽然留在原地却失去地方的经验。再开发完成后，公屋区边界内预计将容纳约 10000 人——比目前约 4400 人的人口大幅增加。改造这个地块需要巨大的空间动荡，随着空间形态为容纳更多建筑而剧烈改变，这一带很可能变得面目全非。居民担心街区无法容纳更高的密度：“现在空间就已经很少了，他们还要再塞进几千人，什么地方都不会有了”（Eileen，租户行动小组会议，2017 年 6 月）。居民担心他们无法把随身携带的空间融入这个新的高密度空间；他们害怕空间的重新安排不会帮助他们重建与地方的联系，害怕自己会在新开发的空间里格格不入，尽管在物理上仍在原地。

4. Temporal Displacements4. 时间失所

In this section, we interrogate how objective and subjective notions of time are recruited and deployed by different actors in Waterloo. Temporal aspects are rarely interrogated in processes of urban change (Rogers, 2014), although we note that work by Elliott-Cooper et al. (2019), Lees and Ferreri (2016), and Pain (2019) has begun to pay attention to the temporal aspects of new urban renewal processes and instances of displacement. The Waterloo redevelopment is characterized by a long and uncertain time frame. The government’s initial indication for a time frame for the completion of the redevelopment was 15–20 years (NSW FACS, 2015). Initially, thanks to unclear wording around a “right to return,” residents were given the impression they might have to move away for the duration of the redevelopment. Further, the initial letter received by residents from the Department of Family and Community Services (now renamed Department of Communities and Justice) indicated that relocations of tenants would begin in mid-2017 (NSW FACS, 2015). No plans for relocation have yet been shared with tenants, indicating that, at the time of writing in early 2020, the residents are still some way from having certainty around relocation time frames.

本节追问不同行动者在 Waterloo 如何调用和运用客观与主观的时间观念。在城市变迁过程中，时间方面很少被追问（Rogers, 2014），不过我们注意到，Elliott-Cooper 等（2019）、Lees 和 Ferreri（2016）以及 Pain（2019）的研究已开始关注新的城市更新过程和失所事例中的时间方面。Waterloo 再开发的特点是时间框架漫长而不确定。政府最初给出的再开发完成时间框架是 15–20 年（NSW FACS, 2015）。起初，由于关于“返回权”的措辞含糊，居民得到的印象是他们可能要在再开发期间搬走。此外，居民从家庭与社区服务部（现更名为社区与司法部）收到的第一封信表示，租户搬迁将于 2017 年年中开始（NSW FACS, 2015）。至今没有任何搬迁计划与租户分享，这表明在本文写作时的 2020 年初，居民离获得搬迁时间框架的确定性还很远。

The long time frame may mean that many residents will never see the end of the redevelopment: the upheaval and change will be the constant that defines the last decades for many elderly residents. Bill, an older tenant representative, is concerned that “if older people have to move from the estate they will never come back” (Waterloo redevelopment group meeting, March 2017). Lana, reflecting on the long time frame for redevelopment and her own age, said “it’ll be 30 years until this starts...I won’t be here [in] 30 years, maybe I’ll not need [to] worry [about the redevelopment]?” (Waterloo redevelopment group meeting, April 2017). Residents appear to struggle with the long time frame, convinced that they will not outlive the redevelopment. The long time frames do not fit with people’s ability to imagine and understand their own lives (e.g., the time they take with them), leading to a great deal of uncertainty and anxiety, particularly among older residents who fear they will live their final decades in uncertain circumstances. Further, these long time frames represent ongoing, protracted struggles that may cause what Pain (2019) describes as “chronic urban trauma,” a slow violence inflicted over the long term.

漫长的时间框架可能意味着许多居民永远看不到再开发结束：动荡和变化将成为许多老年居民最后几十年里不变的底色。年长的租户代表 Bill 担心“如果老人不得不搬离公屋区，他们就再也回不来了”（Waterloo 再开发小组会议，2017 年 3 月）。Lana 想到再开发漫长的时间框架和自己的年龄时说：“要 30 年才开始……30 年后我已经不在了，也许我不用（为再开发）担心了？”（Waterloo 再开发小组会议，2017 年 4 月）。居民似乎难以承受这么长的时间框架，确信自己活不过再开发。漫长的时间框架与人们想象和理解自己人生的能力不相契合（即他们随身携带的时间），导致大量的不确定和焦虑，尤其是在担心自己将在不确定中度过最后几十年的老年居民中。此外，这些漫长的时间框架代表着持续、拖延的斗争，可能造成 Pain（2019）所说的“慢性城市创伤”（chronic urban trauma），一种长期施加的缓慢暴力。

Timeline uncertainty is a key problem in this experience of displacement for residents (Rogers, 2014), and there are several facets to this temporal uncertainty: there is a lack of clarity about when relocations will start, and when the redevelopment will be finished. Temporal uncertainty appears to add to the experience of alienation from place—attachments are, in a way, put on hold, and residents appear afraid to act in certain ways because of uncertainty over the duration of their stay.

时间表的不确定是居民这种失所经验中的一个关键问题（Rogers, 2014），这种时间不确定性有好几个方面：不清楚搬迁何时开始，也不清楚再开发何时结束。时间不确定性似乎加剧了与地方的疏离：依恋在某种意义上被搁置了，居民因为不确定自己能住多久而似乎不敢以某些方式行事。

Bill notes that because of the uncertain time frame,

Bill 指出，由于时间框架不确定，

there is confusion in the community about whether the redevelopment is going to happen or not. . .people are wondering when things are going to happen, there’s a lot of uncertainty. There was the announcement in December 2015, people were told they would begin moving in 2017, now it’s been put off again. (Waterloo redevelopment group meeting, March 2017)

社区里对再开发会不会发生有很多困惑……人们在想事情什么时候会发生，有很多不确定。2015 年 12 月宣布的，人们被告知 2017 年开始搬，现在又推迟了。（Waterloo 再开发小组会议，2017 年 3 月）

租户代表 Bill 在 Waterloo 再开发小组会议上的原话（2017 年 3 月），经作者田野笔记记录。是作者用来支持"时间失所"论点的证据，与作者立场一致。

Valerie, another tenant representative, talked to us about the impact of the uncertainty on the residents:

另一位租户代表 Valerie 向我们谈到不确定性对居民的影响：

there’s so much uncertainty. I’ve got people coming up to me asking if they should paint their apartment, but they don’t know how long they’re going to be here for. People have so many questions. I don’t have the answers. People just want to know how long they’ll be here for. It’s very hard, facing this and not knowing when you’ll need to move. (capacity building workshop, May 2017)

不确定的东西太多了。有人来问我该不该粉刷自己的公寓，可他们不知道自己还能在这里住多久。人们有那么多问题，我没有答案。人们只想知道自己还能在这里住多久。面对这些、又不知道什么时候得搬，真的很难。（能力建设工作坊，2017 年 5 月）

租户代表 Valerie 在能力建设工作坊上的原话（2017 年 5 月）。"该不该粉刷公寓"这个细节，是作者说明"依恋被搁置"的关键证据，与作者立场一致。

Residents feel an inability to plan for the maintenance and upkeep of their homes; they remain unsure about whether to invest energy, time, and emotional resources into maintaining home places and their relationship to them (i.e., the time tenants take with them has been ruptured).

居民感到无法规划家的维护和保养；他们不确定是否该为维护家园和自己与家园的关系投入精力、时间和情感资源（即租户随身携带的时间已经断裂）。

There are also concerns among residents about the post-redevelopment situation, including the formation of new community and social ties. Incumbent residents are concerned that newcomers will not have the same long-term commitments to place that they have—that the newcomers will carry a different time and space with them in this place. Corinne says of these newcomers: “they’ll all be rich international students, the students will never be part of the community—they’re temporary” (tenant action group meeting, March 2017). Establishing new ties in a redeveloped neighborhood takes a great many years: for example, Clampet-Lundquist (2004) describes how, even 2–3 years after relocation, relocated tenants had failed to establish the kinds of meaningful connections that might help reduce their feelings of isolation and vulnerability. This is further complicated when, as in the case of Waterloo, some residents may be relocated several times, and new neighbors in private developments may not move in for many years after the redevelopment project begins. Clampet-Lundquist describes how residents felt themselves to be in a kind of temporal limbo that was preventing them from establishing roots and ties in their new neighborhood, given that they perceived their situation as temporary (Clampet-Lundquist, 2004, p. 437). Residents of Waterloo, especially the elderly and frail, are fearful that they will not live beyond the time it takes to reestablish community connections in their new neighborhood. As the redevelopment proceeds over years and decades to come, their ability to maintain their connection to place and to take their space with them across the long redevelopment timeline will be continuously eroded.

居民也对再开发之后的情形感到担忧，包括新的社区和社会纽带的形成。现有居民担心新来者不会有他们那样对地方的长期承诺——新来者会在这里带着不同的时间和空间。Corinne 这样说这些新来者：“他们会全是有钱的国际学生，学生永远不会成为社区的一部分——他们是临时的”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 3 月）。在再开发后的街区建立新的纽带需要很多年：例如，Clampet-Lundquist（2004）描述了被搬迁的租户即使在搬迁 2–3 年后，也未能建立起有助于减轻孤立感和脆弱感的那种有意义的联系。在 Waterloo 的情况下，情况更加复杂，因为一些居民可能被搬迁好几次，而私人开发里的新邻居可能在再开发项目开始多年后才搬进来。Clampet-Lundquist 描述了居民如何感到自己处于一种时间上的悬置状态，由于他们认为自己的处境是暂时的，这种悬置阻碍他们在新街区扎根和建立纽带（Clampet-Lundquist, 2004, p. 437）。Waterloo 的居民，尤其是年老体弱者，担心自己活不到在新街区重建社区联系所需的那段时间。随着再开发在未来几年乃至几十年推进，他们维持与地方的联系、在漫长的再开发时间线上带着自己的空间前行的能力，将不断被侵蚀。

5. Social, Cultural, and Economic Replacements5. 社会、文化与经济的替换

In this section, we interrogate how the objective and subjective notions of time and space outlined above are shaping social, cultural, and economic replacement in Waterloo. The estate renewal policies being implemented across NSW involve explicit plans to improve social mix, creating “new mixed communities” (NSW FACS, 2018) through bringing more professionals and middle-class families to the area. The government has indicated its intention to secure a 70:30 ratio of private to social housing on the estate, indicating that social housing residents will be greatly outnumbered on the new estate and that a large number of newcomers is likely (NSW FACS, 2018). Wholesale sociocultural upheaval is not, then, an unintended side-effect but rather the very point of the estate redevelopment at Waterloo. Social upheaval and neighborhood upgrading is of course common in instances of gentrification-induced displacement, where working-class residents are evicted from an area because of a range of pressures, and middle-class households take their place (Marcuse, 1985; Wacquant, 2008). Lees notes that the stigmatization of working-class and “underclass” residents of public housing estates through stereotyping and pathologizing tenants is a critical component of the process of implementing the new urban renewal, helping to justify their ultimate expulsion from valuable urban areas (Lees, 2013, p. 927).

本节追问上文概述的客观与主观的时间和空间观念，如何塑造 Waterloo 的社会、文化和经济替换。在 NSW 推行的公屋区更新政策包含明确的改善社会混合的计划，通过引入更多专业人士和中产阶级家庭，创造“新的混合社区”（NSW FACS, 2018）。政府已表明有意在公屋区实现私人与社会住房 70:30 的比例，这意味着社会住房居民在新公屋区中将被远远超过，而且很可能会有大量新来者（NSW FACS, 2018）。因此，社会文化的全面动荡并不是 Waterloo 公屋区再开发的意外副作用，而恰恰是其目的所在。社会动荡和街区升级当然常见于绅士化引发失所的情形，即工人阶级居民因一系列压力被逐出一个地区，中产阶级家庭取而代之（Marcuse, 1985; Wacquant, 2008）。Lees 指出，通过刻板化和病理化租户，对公屋区工人阶级和“下层阶级”居民的污名化，是推行新城市更新过程的关键组成部分，有助于为最终把他们逐出有价值的城市地区提供正当性（Lees, 2013, p. 927）。

A planned uplift in population density on the Waterloo site is what allows this simultaneous remaining-in-place and sociocultural displacement. With thousands of new homes and residents planned for the area, residents will remain in place while an influx of new buildings and households occurs around them, transforming the area’s social make-up without the incumbent residents moving. Given that this enormous upheaval will come despite a lack of physical relocation outside of the neighborhood, these events invite us to understand sociocultural displacement in new ways. The notion of replacement of the times and spaces tenants take with them seems particularly useful here to understand a situation in which incumbent residents remain in place while an overhaul of the neighborhood’s entire social fabric is underway.

Waterloo 地块计划中的人口密度提升，正是使这种“留在原地”与社会文化失所同时发生的原因。该地区计划新建数千套住房、迎来数千名新居民，现有居民将留在原地，而新的建筑和住户在他们周围涌入，在现有居民没有搬走的情况下改变该地区的社会构成。鉴于这一巨大动荡将在没有被迁出街区的情况下到来，这些事件促使我们以新的方式理解社会文化失所。“替换租户随身携带的时间和空间”这一概念，在这里似乎特别有助于理解这样一种情形：现有居民留在原地，而街区的整个社会肌理正在被彻底翻新。

The effects of such processes are not evenly felt across the community, but have gendered, classed, and racialized dimensions. In particular, our fieldwork highlighted the racialized and classed dimensions of the effects of the renewal process, with incumbent tenants describing the renewal process as both “poor cleansing” (e.g., class effect) and “ethnic cleansing” (e.g., Aboriginal or multicultural—racial effect) of their place. In this section we describe the ways in which these classed and racialized effects are felt.

这些过程的影响在社区中并不均匀，而是带有性别、阶级和种族的维度。尤其是，我们的田野工作凸显了更新过程影响中的种族与阶级维度，现有租户把更新过程描述为对他们这个地方的“穷人清洗”（阶级效应）和“种族清洗”（原住民或多元文化——种族效应）。本节描述这些阶级和种族效应是如何被感受到的。

A particular concern exists among the local Aboriginal community for the loss or fragmentation of their community. The local Aboriginal community has undergone several successive displacements from the Redfern-Waterloo area (Shaw, 2000). Despite this, it remains a neighborhood of great importance for Aboriginal people across the country: “whatever happens in Redfern ripples across the country in other Aboriginal communities; this is so important as a place for all Aboriginal people” (Lauren, action group meeting, February 2017).

本地原住民社区尤其担心自己社区的丧失或破碎。本地原住民社区已经历了从 Redfern-Waterloo 地区的多次接连失所（Shaw, 2000）。尽管如此，这里仍是对全国原住民极为重要的街区：“Redfern 发生的任何事都会波及全国其他原住民社区；这里作为一个地方对所有原住民都太重要了”（Lauren，行动小组会议，2017 年 2 月）。

This ongoing pattern of displacements might best be understood in terms of what Fullilove and Wallace (2011) described as serial displacement; and it is certainly a product of the Australian manifestation of racial capitalism (Rogers, 2016a). Focusing on the experiences of African Americans in inner-city areas, Fullilove and Wallace elucidate a number of serious consequences that they find result from such processes of policy-induced serial displacement, including increased community disfunction, social fragmentation, poor health, and increasing experiences of interpersonal and structural violence (Fullilove & Wallace, 2011). Anderson et al., 2018, p. 651) note that contemporary urban housing struggles are understood by many Aboriginal community members as an ongoing pattern of upheaval and displacement, colonization, and dispossession (see Sherwood-Spring & Monro, 2018).

这种持续的失所模式，或许最好用 Fullilove 和 Wallace（2011）所说的“连续失所”（serial displacement）来理解；它无疑也是澳大利亚版种族资本主义的产物（Rogers, 2016a）。Fullilove 和 Wallace 聚焦非裔美国人在内城的经历，阐明了他们发现的这类政策引发的连续失所过程所带来的一系列严重后果，包括社区功能失调加剧、社会碎片化、健康恶化，以及越来越多的人际暴力和结构性暴力经历（Fullilove & Wallace, 2011）。Anderson 等（2018, p. 651）指出，许多原住民社区成员把当代城市住房斗争理解为一种持续的动荡与失所、殖民与剥夺的模式（见 Sherwood-Spring & Monro, 2018）。

The redevelopment is understood by the local Aboriginal community as another step in the systematic dispossession of their land and violence against their people: “they [the government] are subsuming our community, this is ethnic cleansing...they’re bringing the ethnic cleansing down here [to Waterloo]” (Cathy, action group meeting, March 2017). In Waterloo, there exists a concern that the large influx of new residents will provide a means to transform the local Aboriginal community into a tiny minority with little voice or influence: “at this rate, students will outnumber community members...when I moved to Redfern in the 1970s there were 40,000 [Aboriginal] community members here; now there are 200 of us left...all this gentrification is just code for ethnic cleansing” (Cathy, action group meeting, February 2017). The Aboriginal community feels that displacement and dispersal—and, associated with this, disempowerment—of their community will take place despite the absence of physical removal from the neighborhood.

本地原住民社区把再开发理解为对其土地的系统性剥夺和对其人民之暴力的又一步：“他们（政府）在吞并我们的社区，这是种族清洗……他们把种族清洗带到这里（Waterloo）来了”（Cathy，行动小组会议，2017 年 3 月）。在 Waterloo，人们担心大量新居民的涌入，会成为把本地原住民社区变成一个几乎没有发言权和影响力的极少数群体的手段：“照这样下去，学生会比社区成员还多……我 1970 年代搬到 Redfern 时，这里有 40000 名（原住民）社区成员；现在只剩我们 200 人了……所有这些绅士化不过是种族清洗的暗语”（Cathy，行动小组会议，2017 年 2 月）。原住民社区感到，尽管没有被从街区物理迁走，他们社区的失所和离散——以及与之相伴的失去权力——仍将发生。

Manzo, Kleit, and Couch (2008) note that residents of public housing estates are often engaged in a “shared project of living”—a common experience of “getting by” (p. 1861)—that would be disrupted if the residents were relocated to dispersed housing units across the city. Social housing residents fear they will not be able to resume this shared project in their new communities: reintegrating the relationship to place that Waterloo tenants enjoyed prior to the redevelopment will be made almost impossible by the scale of the planned social upheaval. Residents of Waterloo have expressed concern that the new households, who will be owner-occupiers or private rental tenants, will not become part of the Waterloo community: “the students will never be part of the community, they’re temporary” (Corinne, tenant action group meeting, March 2017). Unlike the Seattle, Washington, residents on whose perspectives Manzo et al. (2008) report, these Waterloo residents will not be physically removed from their neighborhood. Despite this, similar concerns abound regarding whether maintenance of their community will be possible once residents are living in a “new mixed community.” Despite having a different physical form, the sociocultural experience of those staying put in Waterloo looks remarkably similar to those being displaced in Seattle. The tenants’ relation to place will be transformed as incumbent residents experience political and cultural displacement at the hands of new households who will form the majority of residents once the redevelopment is completed.

Manzo、Kleit 和 Couch（2008）指出，公屋区居民常常投入一种“共同的生活计划”——一种“勉强过活”的共同经验（p. 1861）——如果居民被分散迁往城市各处的住房单元，这种计划就会被打断。社会住房居民担心他们无法在新社区里继续这个共同计划：计划中社会动荡的规模，将使 Waterloo 租户在再开发前所享有的与地方的关系几乎不可能重新融合。Waterloo 居民表达了这样的担忧：新住户——自住业主或私人租户——不会成为 Waterloo 社区的一部分：“学生永远不会成为社区的一部分，他们是临时的”（Corinne，租户行动小组会议，2017 年 3 月）。与 Manzo 等（2008）所报告的华盛顿州西雅图居民不同，这些 Waterloo 居民不会被从街区物理迁走。尽管如此，人们对居民住进一个“新的混合社区”后能否维持社区也有类似的担忧。尽管物理形式不同，留在 Waterloo 的人的社会文化经验，与在西雅图被迁走的人的经验惊人地相似。随着现有居民在再开发完成后将成为多数的新住户手中经历政治和文化失所，租户与地方的关系将被改变。

Hyra (2015), in a article focused on a gentrifying neighborhood in Washington, DC, describes the political and cultural displacement that incumbent residents experience upon the influx of higher income newcomers. Incumbent residents experience a decrease in their political power as newcomers—often with stronger political capital, higher education levels, or more influence—exert greater influence on political processes and community. Cultural practices, too, may be threatened: previous research has documented that the types of cultural practices that define life in social housing for many—hanging out on stoops, hosting large neighborhood barbecues—are seen as alien to middle-class practices, and are often stamped out by the management of mixed-income communities (Fraser, Burns, Bazuin, & Oakley, 2012). Fraser et al. (2012) report that private residents view practices such as sitting out as indicators that the social order could deteriorate over time because of poor behavior on the part of social housing residents, and so new residents tend to take measures to prevent these behaviors. Cathy, a public housing tenant in Waterloo, notes that people have grown accustomed to living in the Waterloo community, and that the community is very tolerant of its members: “this is a unique place, there’s nowhere else like this.... We live together quite harmoniously compared to other places” (tenant action group meeting, June 2017). Incumbent residents are concerned that new members of the community might look down on social housing residents or might be intolerant of their behavior, making it difficult for residents to continue their cultural and social practices.

Hyra（2015）在一篇聚焦华盛顿特区一个正在绅士化街区的文章中，描述了现有居民在高收入新来者涌入时经历的政治和文化失所。随着新来者——往往拥有更强的政治资本、更高的教育水平或更大影响力——对政治过程和社区施加更大影响，现有居民的政治权力下降。文化实践也可能受到威胁：此前的研究记录了，许多人社会住房生活中的那些标志性文化实践——在门廊台阶上闲坐、举办大型街坊烧烤——被视为与中产阶级实践格格不入，常常被混合收入社区的管理方取缔（Fraser, Burns, Bazuin, & Oakley, 2012）。Fraser 等（2012）报告说，私人居民把坐在外面这类实践视为社会秩序可能因社会住房居民的不良行为而随时间恶化的迹象，因此新居民倾向于采取措施阻止这些行为。Waterloo 的公屋租户 Cathy 指出，人们已经习惯了在 Waterloo 社区生活，这个社区对其成员非常包容：“这是一个独特的地方，没有别的地方像这里……和别处相比，我们相处得很融洽”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 6 月）。现有居民担心社区的新成员可能会看不起社会住房居民，或无法容忍他们的行为，使居民难以继续他们的文化和社会实践。

Familiarity, networks, and connections are likely to be disrupted even though the residents will not move from the neighborhood and most of the members of their networks will remain, physically, in the area. The experiences of Waterloo residents point to a need to understand these networks not only as rooted in place but as rooted in a particular arrangement of things in place: when those arrangements are irreversibly disrupted, we cannot expect the networks and connections that previously existed to remain the same, even if they are located in the same physical space. The presence of several thousand new residents, with very different household incomes, education levels, political values, and cultural practices to the incumbent residents of the public housing estate, will get in the way of current residents as they attempt to resituate their attachment to place on the redeveloped estate.

即使居民不会搬离街区、他们网络中的大多数成员在物理上仍留在该地区，熟悉感、网络和联系也很可能被打断。Waterloo 居民的经验表明，需要把这些网络理解为不仅扎根于地方，而且扎根于地方中某种特定的事物安排：当这些安排被不可逆地打乱时，我们不能指望先前存在的网络和联系保持不变，即使它们位于同一个物理空间。数千名新居民的到来——他们的家庭收入、教育水平、政治价值观和文化实践都与公屋区现有居民截然不同——将妨碍现有居民在再开发后的公屋区重新安放自己对地方的依恋。

By allowing public housing tenants to remain in place, the state government might hope to avoid the class conflicts inherent in processes of state-led renewal that lead to the physical relocation of poorer residents. However, the upheaval of their low-income community and the subsuming of their space by middle-class households appears to be much the same experience for tenants, despite remaining in place. Residents see the neighborhood change as a sign that they, being poor and disadvantaged, are the subject of a policy that aims to minimize their presence in the neighborhood through dilution—such that in addition to claims of “ethnic cleansing” mentioned above, they feel the redevelopment is an attempt to reduce them to a minority or remove them from the area altogether: “it’s not just ethnic cleansing, it’s poor cleansing” (Catriona, action group meeting, September 2017).

通过允许公屋租户留在原地，州政府或许希望避免导致较穷居民被物理迁走的国家主导更新过程中固有的阶级冲突。然而，对租户来说，他们低收入社区的动荡、他们的空间被中产阶级家庭吞并，似乎是几乎相同的经验，尽管他们留在原地。居民把街区变化看作一种迹象，表明他们作为穷人和弱势者，是一项旨在通过“稀释”把他们在街区中的存在降到最低的政策的对象——因此，除了上面提到的“种族清洗”说法，他们还觉得再开发是试图把他们变成少数，或者把他们完全从该地区移走：“这不只是种族清洗，这是穷人清洗”（Catriona，行动小组会议，2017 年 9 月）。

The Communities Plus program—the banner under which the redevelopment is planned and implemented—aims to implement “social mixing” (NSW FACS, 2018). The underlying logic of social mix programs is that they will improve the life chances of incumbent residents through proximity to wealthier households with supposedly greater social capital. At core, this is about changing the space tenants take with them and changing what the buildings ideologically stand for in the minds of tenants. In the minds of residents such as Hannah, this is experienced as an assault on their community identity: “[this redevelopment is] slum clearance—we’re to be cleansed by living next to yuppies” (tenant action group meeting, May 2017).

Communities Plus 计划——再开发据以规划和实施的旗号——旨在推行“社会混合”（NSW FACS, 2018）。社会混合计划的根本逻辑是，通过接近据称拥有更多社会资本的富裕家庭，改善现有居民的生活机会。从根本上说，这是要改变租户随身携带的空间，改变这些楼在租户心中在意识形态上所代表的东西。在 Hannah 这样的居民看来，这被体验为对他们社区认同的攻击：“（这次再开发）就是清理贫民窟——要让我们靠住在雅皮士旁边来被‘净化’”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 5 月）。

Hyra describes an experience of economic displacement despite remaining in place (2015, p. 1760): in the gentrifying U Street neighborhood of Washington, DC, new businesses such as “sit-down restaurants” replaced the go-go nightclubs that played Caribbean music popular with the local community, and the changed services and retail alienated incumbent residents from their neighborhood (Hyra, 2015, p. 1760). Similarly, in Waterloo, residents are concerned about the economic transformation of the area, including a shift in the types of local businesses in the area. Changes in businesses will likely have implications for the affordability of the area as well as the distance residents will have to travel to access key services, in particular free or affordable medical care, mental health care, and pharmaceuticals. In Waterloo, as Corinne notes, “services are targeted to areas on the basis of high need, but Waterloo won’t be ‘high needs’ any more after the redevelopment, so the funding will go elsewhere and we won’t have the services we need” (tenant action group meeting, May 2017).

Hyra 描述了一种尽管留在原地却经历经济失所的经验（2015, p. 1760）：在华盛顿特区正在绅士化的 U Street 街区，“正式餐厅”这样的新商家取代了播放深受本地社区喜爱的加勒比音乐的 go-go 夜店，服务和零售的改变使现有居民与自己的街区产生疏离（Hyra, 2015, p. 1760）。同样，在 Waterloo，居民担心该地区的经济转变，包括本地商家类型的变化。商家的变化很可能影响该地区的可负担性，以及居民为获得关键服务——尤其是免费或可负担的医疗、精神健康护理和药品——所需走的距离。在 Waterloo，正如 Corinne 指出的，“服务是根据需求高低分配到各地区的，但再开发之后 Waterloo 就不再是‘高需求’地区了，所以资金会流向别处，我们就得不到需要的服务了”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 5 月）。

Economic transformation at a larger scale is also a concern to residents. Private leasing or ownership of land, and the private development of buildings, represents a major shift in economic function in Waterloo. The sale or leasing of public land at the site is not experienced by residents as an inconsequential shift in ownership but as a major transformation of the economic arrangements that govern their space. Residents feel that the sale of public assets is “just not right” (tenant action group meeting, May 2017) and that it will set the area on an irreversible course of change.

更大尺度上的经济转变也让居民担忧。土地的私人租赁或所有，以及建筑的私人开发，代表着 Waterloo 经济功能的重大转变。居民并不把该地块公有土地的出售或出租体验为无关紧要的所有权变化，而是体验为支配他们空间的经济安排的重大转变。居民觉得出售公共资产“就是不对的”（租户行动小组会议，2017 年 5 月），会让这一带走上一条不可逆转的变化之路。

As land is transferred, whether through sales, leases, or management agreements, from public to private ownership, the arrangements that underpin tenants’ relationship to this place change too. Tenants’ relationship to place is both consciously and unconsciously mediated through the public ownership of space in Waterloo that facilitates and protects their access to this space, at least to a certain extent. Transfer of land to private ownership threatens to change access to and provision of both public space and community and private spaces, and will disrupt tenants’ ability to maintain connections to place despite remaining within the neighborhood.

随着土地通过出售、出租或管理协议从公有转为私有，支撑租户与这个地方之关系的安排也随之改变。租户与地方的关系，有意识或无意识地，都以 Waterloo 空间的公有所有权为中介，这种公有所有权在一定程度上促成并保护着他们对这个空间的使用。土地转为私有，可能改变公共空间以及社区和私人空间的使用与供给，并将打断租户在留在街区内的同时维持与地方联系的能力。

6. Conclusion6. 结论

This analysis prompts several policy and scholarly questions. In the public policy sphere, there is a need to pay more attention to the spatial scale in question when seeking to understand experiences of displacement. Government and policy discourse around displacement tends to focus on the neighborhood as the scale at which displacement occurs. This focus, however, similarly silences and ignores the experiences of those who experience displacement within their neighborhood: those low-income residents who find themselves unable to reintegrate the space they take with them as they are subjected to processes of urban renewal, despite remaining in the same postcode.

这项分析引出若干政策和学术问题。在公共政策领域，需要在试图理解失所经验时更多关注所涉及的空间尺度。政府和政策关于失所的话语，往往把街区当作失所发生的尺度。然而，这种聚焦同样会噤声和忽视那些在自己街区内部经历失所的人的经验：那些在城市更新过程中无法重新融合自己随身携带之空间的低收入居民，尽管他们仍住在同一个邮编区。

Policymakers in Australia are beginning to accept that housing inequity is a structural issue, but there is still much debate about which structure to focus on. State-led redevelopments that allow residents to remain in place tend to be presented by governments as less egregious than processes that physically remove residents. However, it is not the benevolence of the state that grants residents the right to stay in place, but the characteristics of land values across space in the global city that allow for an increase in density, providing room for both social and private housing on redeveloped sites. Clearly, land-use policy, public housing policy, and broader taxation settings around home ownership in Australia represent an important structural context for addressing housing inequity. But embodied displacement is also a heavily racialized and classed process in Redfern/Waterloo, and this necessarily draws in the policy levers around Aboriginal land rights and Aboriginal self-determination, therefore making the violent history of Aboriginal dispossession of the land an important contemporary structural context too. As this shows, there is no easy structural fix to housing inequity in Australia, with our analysis suggesting that the sites for policy change are expansive and it may in fact be problematic to target only a few key policy areas to address the housing problem in Sydney.

澳大利亚的政策制定者开始接受住房不公平是一个结构性问题，但对于该聚焦于哪种结构仍有很多争论。允许居民留在原地的国家主导再开发，往往被政府呈现为比把居民物理迁走的过程危害更小。然而，赋予居民留在原地之权利的，并不是国家的仁慈，而是全球城市中地价的空间特征，它允许提高密度，为在再开发地块上同时容纳社会住房和私人住房提供了空间。显然，澳大利亚的土地利用政策、公共住房政策以及围绕自有住房的更广泛税收设置，是解决住房不公平的一个重要结构性语境。但具身失所在 Redfern/Waterloo 也是一个高度种族化和阶级化的过程，这必然涉及原住民土地权和原住民自决的政策杠杆，从而使原住民土地被暴力剥夺的历史也成为一个重要的当代结构性语境。由此可见，澳大利亚的住房不公平没有简单的结构性解决办法；我们的分析表明，政策变革的场所十分广泛，只针对少数几个关键政策领域来解决悉尼的住房问题，实际上可能是有问题的。

In terms of scholarly questions, the experience of residents in Waterloo should serve as a reminder to urban scholars examining urbanization and renewal processes to incorporate a nuanced understanding of time, space, and place as we consider eviction and displacement in the global city. Understanding displacement only as movement through Cartesian space serves to ignore the experiences and silence the perspectives of low-income residents who are displaced—whose relationship to space is fundamentally, and perhaps irreparably, disrupted— by processes of urban renewal, racial capitalism, etc., despite being granted the right to remain in place.

就学术问题而言，Waterloo 居民的经验应当提醒研究城市化和更新过程的城市学者，在考察全球城市中的驱逐和失所时，要纳入对时间、空间和地方的细致理解。只把失所理解为在笛卡尔空间中的移动，就会忽视那些失所的低收入居民的经验、使他们的视角噤声——他们与空间的关系被城市更新、种族资本主义等过程从根本上、也许是无法弥补地打乱了，尽管他们被授予了留在原地的权利。

We explored this through the notion of embodied displacement to attend to a more nuanced understanding of place for residents of social housing undergoing upheaval because of redevelopment. As documented by Hulse and Reynolds (2017), this is a phenomenon not limited to those in areas undergoing renewal, but also experienced by other residents of the global city—particularly those living in rental housing and in precarious circumstances. The analysis we have undertaken here recognizes that an absence of physical relocation from a neighborhood does not equate to an absence of displacement. We do not suggest that embodied displacement supplant displacement in urban scholarship, but rather that it be used in addition to displacement to address and capture the diversity of experiences of loss of place (both temporal and spatial) that are inflicted upon residents —in particular, low-income renters—of the global city.

我们通过“具身失所”这一概念探讨了这一点，以便更细致地理解因再开发而经历动荡的社会住房居民的地方。正如 Hulse 和 Reynolds（2017）所记录的，这一现象不限于正在更新的地区的人，全球城市的其他居民——尤其是住在租赁住房里、处境不稳定的人——也会经历。我们在这里所做的分析承认，没有被从街区物理迁走，并不等于没有失所。我们并不主张用具身失所取代城市研究中的失所概念，而是主张把它作为失所的补充，用来处理和捕捉施加在全球城市居民——尤其是低收入租户——身上的失去地方（在时间和空间两方面）的经验的多样性。

If urbanization is a process, rather than an event, then we should approach public housing redevelopments as a part of this ongoing history and not simply a renewal event that needs to be managed in the present. In Sydney, the move from the physical relocation of public housing residents to the social, cultural, and economic replacement of almost every facet of their lives should not be taken as isolated events occurring at different points in time. Rather, they should be understood as steps in a long and ongoing state-led process of urban transformation, and for low-income Aboriginal people as a process of ongoing colonization and land dispossession. We need to think about how the people of our cities—and especially the most vulnerable in our cities—will or will not adapt to this change. Indeed, some of the most vulnerable in our cities may not have the skills, knowledge, capacities, or resources to adapt. Should we simply leave them to the highly classed and racialized neoliberal forces of the city, or do we need to create a new structural context within which every member of our communities can deal with the transformation of our cities?

如果城市化是一个过程而不是一个事件，那么我们就应当把公屋再开发看作这段持续历史的一部分，而不只是一个需要在当下加以管理的更新事件。在悉尼，从对公屋居民的物理迁移，到对他们生活几乎每个方面的社会、文化和经济替换，不应被看作发生在不同时间点上的孤立事件。相反，它们应被理解为一个漫长而持续的、国家主导的城市转型过程中的步骤；对低收入原住民来说，则是一个持续的殖民和土地剥夺过程。我们需要思考，我们城市里的人——尤其是城市中最脆弱的人——会如何或不会如何适应这种变化。事实上，我们城市中一些最脆弱的人，可能没有适应所需的技能、知识、能力或资源。我们是应该把他们简单地交给城市中高度阶级化和种族化的新自由主义力量，还是需要创造一种新的结构性语境，让我们社区里的每一个成员都能应对城市的转型？

概念 在地失所（emplaced displacement）

本文核心概念：人还住在原地，但与地方的关系（空间、时间、社会文化）已被打断。作者也称"具身失所"（embodied displacement）。课程对照：Davidson 的间接置换；Marcuse 的排斥性置换。用在论文"置换"一节。

概念 失所 / 置换（displacement）

传统定义强调人被迫搬离住所（Marcuse 1985）。本文要扩展这个定义：失所的是"与地方的关系"，不只是身体的位置。

概念 替换（replacement）

Hamnett (2003) 用它否认绅士化引发置换；本文反其道而用之，依循 Hulse & Reynolds (2017)：没有物理迁移的绅士化。注意两种用法立场相反。

概念 带着空间走（taking space with you）

Keenan (2017) 借 Massey：人移动时会随身带着一个特定的空间（关系、习惯、归属）。本文用它说明再开发如何让租户"随身携带的空间"断裂。

理论/思潮 全球城市（global city）

Sassen 的概念。本文用它解释 Waterloo 为何特殊：靠近 CBD 的高地价使大幅加密成为可能，租户因此才能"留在原地"。课程对照：Introduction 讲义 Sassen (1991)；你提出的 Green Square 对照。

政策/概念 社会混合（social mix）

以 70:30 私人对社会住房重建公屋区。本文第 5 节：社会文化的全面动荡"不是意外副作用，而恰恰是目的"。课程对照：Lees (2008)。

政策/机构 Communities Plus

NSW 公屋再开发计划，Waterloo 的实施旗号。

概念 地价提升（land value uplift）

交通投资带来的土地增值。本文指出再开发决定部分与地铁站选址相关。课程对照：租差。

政策/机构 绿色禁令（green ban）

1970 年代 NSW 建筑工人联合会拒绝拆除工人住房和遗产建筑的行动，叫停了 Redfern-Waterloo 的大拆大建。

概念 慢性城市创伤（chronic urban trauma）

Pain (2019)：长期拖延的城市斗争造成的"缓慢暴力"。本文用来描述漫长、不确定的再开发时间线对老年居民的影响。

概念 连续失所（serial displacement）

Fullilove & Wallace (2011)：同一群体被政策一再迁移。本文用来理解原住民社区从 Redfern-Waterloo 的多次失所。资料对照：Shaw (2000) 关于 Redfern。

概念 政治与文化失所（political and cultural displacement）

Hyra (2015)：新来者带来更强的政治资本和不同的文化规范，原住户在留下的同时失去影响力和生活方式。

概念 污名化（stigmatization）

本文引 Lees (2013)：病理化公屋租户是推行新城市更新的关键环节。课程与资料对照：Sisson (2024)；Zanardo et al. (2024)。
