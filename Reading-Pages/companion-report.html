§2 全球城市

2. The global city: why Waterloo is drawn into the dispute

论文正文 Paper

2.1The strategy that frames Sydney as a global city does not name Waterloo. The state’s 2014 metropolitan strategy defines a Global Economic Corridor that “generates over 41 per cent of the NSW Gross State Product” and lists Green Square among its strategic centres, in line with the global-city idea that a city’s competitiveness rests on central land, infrastructure and the services of a global economy (Sassen 1991; NSW Planning and Environment 2014, 44). Waterloo’s tenants nevertheless live inside this ambition, as Wynne (2018) shows. So the question for the renewal is what happens to the people already living on the land.

2.2The renewal is paid for by selling new private flats, so the price those flats can fetch matters, and that price has risen strongly. The median transfer price of attached dwellings in Sydney rose from $390,000 in June 2006 to $840,000 in June 2026, and that of houses from $495,000 to $1,487,600 (Australian Bureau of Statistics 2026; Figure 1). The rise has paused: Sydney values fell 9.4 per cent in the year to October 2026 but remain 34.5 per cent above their level ten years earlier (Cotality 2026). These figures show the pressure on the plan, not what happens to tenants, and they are for the whole city and the postcode. I found no price or rent figures for the Waterloo site itself.

2.3What brought the pressure to Waterloo was a metro station. The urban design study for Waterloo South calls the station “the catalyst for the renewal” (Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6), and the planning proposal argues that keeping the site as it is would ignore “the potential catalytic effects of the new Metro station” (NSW Land and Housing Corporation [LAHC] 2020, 92). So the pressure arrived through a transport investment and not through the global-city strategy. The reason given for replacing the housing is a different one. Future Directions, a statewide strategy, says that “many estates experience high levels of crime, unemployment, domestic violence, tenancy management problems, poor educational outcomes and associated child protection issues” (NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 24). This describes estates in general, not Waterloo, and section 4 tests it.

伴读报告 Companion

框架

这一节用的是全球城市的框架。按讲义里的说法，Sassen (1991) 认为全球化让生产分散、控制集中，少数城市因此成为管理和协调全球经济的枢纽，它们的竞争力靠中心区的土地、基础设施和高端服务；Brenner and Schmid 的星球城市化则提醒，不要把城市化只看成个别城市的事。这套框架回答的是一个城市尺度的问题：为什么一座城市需要更多、更贵的中心区土地。需要说明的是，这些作者我是据讲义引用的，没有读原文。

案例

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 把这个城市尺度的问题落到一块具体的土地上，并且显示了框架里看不见的一环：压力是怎样到达这块地的。2014 年的大都市战略划出“全球经济走廊”，把 Green Square 列为战略中心，文本里没有点名 Waterloo（NSW Planning and Environment 2014, 44）。而论证更新的文件，把直接的推动力说成地铁站：城市设计研究称车站是“这次更新的催化剂”（Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6），规划建议书说保持现状会忽视车站“潜在的催化效应”（LAHC 2020, 92）。所以，全球城市的压力到达 Waterloo，经过的是一项交通投资，而不是经过那份框定走廊的战略。这是我对这些文件的读法，不是文件自己的话。

赞同·拓展

赞同与拓展

对 Sassen 的方向，案例是赞同的：中心区土地确实被重新估价，悉尼附属住宅的中位转手价从 2006 年 6 月的 39 万澳元涨到 2026 年 6 月的 84 万澳元（Australian Bureau of Statistics 2026）。案例拓展的是机制：全球城市理论说“为什么需要这块地”，案例补上“压力经由什么落到这块地”，答案是基础设施。

局限

理论太线性，Waterloo 不是

框架暗含一个线性的预期：压力上升，重建随之推进。Waterloo 的时间线不是这样的。更新 2015 年宣布，规划路径 2019 年从州转到市议会，2021 年又回到州（Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6；NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 11）。现任部长自己在新闻稿里说，更新“宣布后停滞多年，直到 2022 年才有进展”（Scully 2026）。2026 年还出现了反向的信号：Cotality 的指数显示悉尼房价一年下降 9.4%（Cotality 2026），同年首批约 150 套住宅的围栏却开始搭建（Bowring 2026）。这说明落地的时间不是由价格单独决定的，政府更替和规划权的安排同样重要。需要注意，Scully 的新闻稿是执政方部长的发言，带着“我们 2023 年选举后把愿景变成行动”的叙事，所以我只把它当作官方对时间线的自述，不当作中立的历史。

小结

这一节里，全球城市框架解释了“为什么有压力”，案例补上了“压力经由地铁站传导”，并指出了框架不解释的部分：什么时候落地、由谁推动、以什么形式落地。写进论文时，措辞要用“与……一致”“补充了……”，不能写成“证明”或“推翻”Sassen：一个案例、一类文件，只能说明机制，不能检验理论。

§3 住房

3. Housing: the strategy, and the conflict between home and real estate

论文正文 Paper

3.1From the start the plan has treated Waterloo both as people’s homes and as a way to pay for new housing. In December 2015 the state announced that the estate would be demolished and rebuilt with about 30 per cent social and 70 per cent private housing, the sale of the private dwellings funding the project (Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208), in line with a statewide target of 70:30 (NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 9). Who decides has also changed: in November 2019 the planning route moved from the state to the council (Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6), and in March 2021 the state took the planning authority back (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 11). The planning proposal says the existing properties are “at the end of their economic life” (LAHC 2020, 23), yet the same study gives their average age as 44 years against a benchmark life of 60 (Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6). Madden and Marcuse (2016) call this tension a conflict between home and real estate: housing as lived, social space against housing as an instrument for profit-making.

3.2The promises to tenants grew after 2023, but the planning rules were written before them (Figure 2). The plan approved in 2022 provides 3,012 dwellings, of which 847 are social housing, and its planning controls date from November 2022 (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 37). After a change of government in March 2023, the share of social and affordable housing across the estate was raised from 34 to 50 per cent, tenants were promised the right to return, and relocation was to proceed in small stages (NSW Government 2023). In 2024 the government promised at least six months’ notice and dedicated support, and repeated that all residents would be given the right to return (NSW Government 2024). By 2026 a new concept application proposed about 3,300 homes, at least half of them social or affordable (Scully 2026), and fencing began around the first block of about 150 homes, due for demolition by the end of June (Bowring 2026). So the promises moved ahead of the rules. The floor-area part has since reached the planning documents: the proponent’s 2025 scoping report describes 50 per cent of residential floor area as committed to social and affordable housing (Beam Planning 2025, PDF pp. 4, 20), and the 2026 concept application proposes at least 30 per cent of residential floor area as social housing and no more than 50 per cent as market housing (NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026). None of the three documents I read for this stage mentions a right of return.

3.3The strategy can be sorted into measures that protect the money and measures that protect the tenants’ homes. On the money side, the planning proposal says that the existing planning rules “would not be capable of supporting a feasible redevelopment of the entire site given the costs of demolition and construction” (LAHC 2020, 92), an early proposal offered extra floor space only if 30 per cent of residential floor area were social housing and 20 per cent affordable (NSW Land and Housing Corporation 2021, Attachment A), and the sale of private dwellings carries the cost. On the tenants’ side are the right to return, more social homes in Waterloo South than there are now (Homes NSW 2026) and a promise that Aboriginal people will get at least 15 per cent of the new social and affordable homes (NSW Government 2023). No official document describes the funding in these terms; the sorting is mine. Sections 4.5 and 4.6 ask which side the binding rules protect.

伴读报告 Companion

框架

这一节的框架是住房研究里“家与房地产”的对立。Madden and Marcuse (2016, 4) 把今天的住房描述为：住房作为生活的、社会性的空间，与住房作为盈利的工具，两者之间有冲突，也就是家与房地产之间的冲突（据讲义）。讲义里还有 Aalbers (2016) 的住房金融化：金融动机、金融市场和金融行为者在塑造住房上的作用越来越大，而且结果因情境而异。

案例

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 让这个对立变得具体：同一个计划同时承载两边。一边是房地产：规划建议书说现有规划“不足以支持对整个场地进行可行的重新开发”（LAHC 2020, 92），私人住宅的销售资助整个项目（Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208）。另一边是家：回迁权、比现在更多的 Waterloo South 社会住房（Homes NSW 2026）。更有意思的是，两边是通过一个比例在谈判：2015 年约 30% 社会住房、70% 私人住房（Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208）；一份早期提案只有在 30% 的住宅建筑面积是社会住房、20% 是可负担住房时才给额外楼面积（NSW Land and Housing Corporation 2021, Attachment A）；2022 年定稿的控制是社会住房 26.5%、可负担住房 7.0%（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 7）；2023 年政府更替后，全区社会与可负担住房的比例从 34% 提高到 50%（NSW Government 2023）。对立在这里表现为一个数字在多年里被反复改写。

拓展·深化

拓展与深化

第一，拓展：Madden and Marcuse 的对立通常被读成租户与开发商之间的冲突，Waterloo 显示冲突也发生在国家内部。提出计划的是土地与住房公司，评估它的是规划部门，市议会也提了意见，计划定稿报告的附件列表里有对土地与住房公司和对市议会的分别回应（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, 附件目录）。比例的谈判在报告里留有痕迹：有一条建议要求至少恢复公示版规划建议书里“土地与住房公司地块上至少 30% 的住宅楼面积是社会住房、20% 是可负担住房”，答复是保留 26.5% 和 7.0%，理由是这些最低值与 Gateway 决定一致，后者要求至少 847 套社会住房（同上，PDF p. 30）。2021 年 3 月规划权又收回到州（同上，PDF p. 11），所以同一个国家既是业主，又是提案方，也是规划机关。第二，深化：房屋状况本身成了争论的材料。规划建议书说房产“已到经济寿命的尽头”（LAHC 2020, 23），而同一份研究给出的平均房龄是 44 年，基准寿命是 60 年（Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6）；Zanardo 等 (2024) 则认为，除板楼可能是例外外，大多数建筑只需相对适度的翻新就能延长寿命。也就是说，“家”这一边也要靠对建筑状况的判断来论证。

局限

对立不是非黑即白

理论把两边写成对立，案例显示的是混合：保护租户的“家”，在这里要靠出售房地产才能出钱。Homes NSW 说新的 Waterloo South 会有比现在更多的社会住房（Homes NSW 2026），按计划的数字是 749 套增加到 847 套，只多 98 套（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 8, 37；差值由计算得出），而占比却从约 86% 降到约 28%。同一个计划，按套数数是增长，按占比数是稀释，所以“房地产逻辑赢了还是家赢了”取决于你用哪个分母，理论本身没有给出判断的标准。另外，Aalbers 的金融化概念通常指机构投资者、上市的房地产信托之类。我读到的 Waterloo 文件里，资金来自出售私人住宅，没有看到机构投资者，所以我在 §4.3 用“金融化”一词是宽松的用法，这是我对讲义概念的一个局限，不是 Aalbers 本人的结论。比例也在继续变：2026 年的概念申请提出社会住房至少占住宅建筑面积的 30%、市场住房至多 50%（NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026），开发方 2025 年的规划说明报告把住宅建筑面积的 50% 写成已承诺给社会和可负担住房（Beam Planning 2025, PDF pp. 4, 20）。所以“房地产逻辑赢了还是家赢了”不是一次定下的结果，而是被一路改写的数字。这里的判断依据我读到的文件；环境影响报告和公报版规则我没读，如果它们改了比例或把回迁写进规则，这里要跟着改。

写作规范

在论文里，这一节只能说：案例“与”家与房地产的对立“一致”，并“显示”这个对立由比例和分母来承载；“冲突在国家内部”是我基于文件分工做的推论，要写“我读作”。

§4 隔离

4. Segregation: testing the remedy against the chain

论文正文 Paper

4.1The state’s case for renewal rests on an argument that segregation research also makes: disadvantage that clusters in one place reproduces itself. Tammaru et al. (2021) describe vicious circles of segregation as the product of connected social and spatial inequalities in different life domains: advantage breeds advantage and disadvantage breeds disadvantage. Five steps can be distinguished (Figure 3): disadvantage clusters spatially, neighbourhood effects follow, poor perceptions shape who moves, segregation spreads to other domains such as schools and work, and it becomes immobility across the life course and across generations (Rosengren 2026). Future Directions follows the same chain. It finds disadvantage concentrated in estates, says that its effects “can be passed on through multiple generations, reinforcing the cycle of disadvantage” (NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 24), and proposes mixed tenure, with a 70:30 split between private and social dwellings “to enable more integrated communities” (PDF p. 9). The rest of this section tests that remedy step by step.

4.2On step 1 the plan works: social housing falls from 86 per cent of the site’s dwellings to 28 per cent. Waterloo South now has 749 social and 120 private dwellings, and the approved plan has 847 social dwellings among 3,012 (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 8, 37; shares calculated from these figures; Figure 4). This is dilution by social mixing, which makes concentrated disadvantage less visible. The same plan can also be described as growth, 847 social dwellings against 749, so a document can call it dilution or growth depending on whether it counts share or number. The precedent that the urban design study uses for density has far less social housing: Green Square Town Centre is tabulated with 104 social and affordable dwellings among 3,308, about 3 per cent (Turner Studio & Turf 2020, App. 6, 463–464). Either way, step 1 is the part of the remedy that the planning rules fix (section 4.5).

4.3The 70:30 remedy was not designed from evidence about what mixing achieves. Khor et al. (2023, 77), citing Darcy and Rogers (2019), trace the ratio to Bonnyrigg, a suburban estate, where social-mixing outcomes “was not a determinant of decision-making”, and Shrestha, Ruming and Rogers (2025) find that Sydney’s estate-renewal models have moved “from periphery, through middle ring, towards core”. So a remedy first used on suburban estates has now reached the inner city. I found no counts of returning tenants and no measures of neighbourhood effects for these estates. My inference, which these authors do not state, is that the ratio was set by what financing required, a case of the financialisation of housing (Aalbers 2016), and so the rules enforce a quantity of tenure and not an outcome.

4.4Where displaced tenants go decides whether the cluster is dissolved or only moved. Current tenants have moved into other public housing, at least 70 of them into the new homes beside the Waterloo metro station (Bowring 2026). The government names two local destinations, the 70 social homes at the Metro Quarter and about 95 more at Elizabeth Street, Redfern (Homes NSW 2026), and in 2023 it presented Elizabeth Street as a way to rehouse Waterloo South tenants locally (NSW Government 2023; Figure 5). At Bonnyrigg, by contrast, the first wave of a panel study found that households who had left the estate, a small group of interviews, had resettled in suburban settings where public housing is not the majority tenure (Pinnegar, Liu and Randolph 2013), so that finding cannot be applied to Waterloo, where the named destinations are other social housing nearby. No document gives the number of tenants at each Waterloo destination. What can be said is that the plan dilutes social housing on the site while also placing social housing nearby.

4.5The planning rules guarantee how much social housing is built, but not who lives in it and not whether tenants can come back. The site-specific controls that the plan finalisation report lists for Waterloo South require no less than 26.5 per cent of residential floor area as social housing, no less than 7.0 per cent as affordable housing, and at least 5,000 square metres for community, health and childcare facilities (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 7). These are minimum amounts of floor area. If a person’s relation to housing shapes their class position, as housing class theory holds (Ruonavaara 2025), such minimums fix which kinds of housing the new buildings contain, not which households hold them. The Design Guide, where the tenure and Aboriginal housing provisions sit, is only to be considered in development applications “but not to elevate the guidance within the design guide to development standards” (PDF p. 7). Its principle that social and affordable housing be provided “so that a socially diverse residential population, representative of all income groups, is created” (Hassell 2022b, PDF p. 22) comes closest to the people the housing is for, and it does not mention return. So the rules say how much social housing there will be, and say nothing about who will live in it or whether tenants can come back.

4.6Return and relocation appear only as statements. The final planning proposal records that the Land and Housing Corporation “has noted” that current residents have a right of return (Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 59), and in assessing the rezoning the department accepted a response stating that “appropriate relocation strategies will be developed” (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 14). The report adds that state significant developments must include a social impact assessment (PDF p. 31), which is done when applying, and neither document requires anyone to track what happens to tenants after they move. The same gap is older than the plan. In the 2019 consultation, residents stressed the importance of communication about “arrangements for relocations and rehousing” (Elton Consulting 2019, PDF p. 10), and in 2022 the answer was that strategies “will be developed”. These controls are also changing. The 2025 evaluation panel report describes a proposal to amend the Sydney local plan by “increasing building heights, redistributing floor space, and introducing flexibility to the application of the design guide” (NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2025a, 1), and the exhibition notice of May 2026 lists amendments to the local plan alongside a concept plan with a minimum of 30 per cent of residential floor area as social housing (NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026). The requirements issued for the concept application in October 2025 ask for a Social Impact Assessment and a Preliminary Social Impact Management Plan “to identify and mitigate impacts from the development on local communities” (NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2025b, PDF p. 9), without naming relocation or return. The proponent’s scoping report says that faster design review will “support tenant relocations directly into new homes” (Beam Planning 2025, PDF p. 27), a statement and not a mechanism. I read these three documents and the 2022 ones; I did not read the environmental impact statement, the social impact assessment or the gazetted rules. In terms of the five steps, the rules reach step 1 and, through the facilities, the services side of step 4. Steps 3 and 5, which concern where people go and what happens to them, depend on promises outside the rules (Table 1).

4.7Steps 2 to 5 are where the evidence is thinnest. Sisson (2024) notes that the concept of neighbourhood effects behind “concentrated disadvantage” “controversially” places the cause in the estate. Hirvonen and Lilius (2019), in a Finnish survey, find only small differences in neighbour ties between house types and a need to develop social ties “also in rental houses”, which suggests that contact across tenures has to be built and does not follow from proximity; the survey is Finnish and does not address mixed tenure. Uncertainty already acts on steps three and five. Wynne and Rogers (2021) argue that the threat of eviction “has already done significant damage to their relationship to place” before any move, and a resident, Julie, said that she heard of the demolition work when she woke to find fences going up, and that her relocation had been delayed (Bowring 2026). Read through the chain, residents neither choose nor time their moves, and the wait is part of the circle. This is my reading; no document measures residents’ mobility or opportunities.

伴读报告 Companion

框架

这一节用的是隔离研究的框架。Tammaru 等 (2021) 把隔离的恶性循环描述为不同生活领域里社会不平等与空间不平等相互关联的产物：优势滋生优势，弱势滋生弱势。讲义里 Rosengren (2026) 列出五个步骤：弱势在空间上聚集，邻里效应随之而来，不佳的观感影响谁会搬迁，隔离扩展到学校、工作等其他领域，最终成为跨越代际的不能流动。这两处我是据讲义引用的。

案例

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 先显示的是：这条链不只是学者的说法，国家自己用它来论证更新。Future Directions 说弱势集中在住宅区里，其影响“可以代代相传，强化弱势的循环”（NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 24），药方是 70:30 的混合租住（同上，PDF p. 9）。其次，案例让每一步的检验变得具体：第 1 步，社会住房在场地住宅中的占比从约 86% 降到约 28%（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 8, 37）；第 3 步，租户被搬到哪里，点名的去向是 Metro Quarter 的 70 套和 Redfern Elizabeth Street 约 95 套（Homes NSW 2026）。第 2、4、5 步，我读到的文件没有测量。

赞同·拓展·深化

赞同、拓展与深化

赞同：Tammaru 的“不同领域相互关联”在 Waterloo 里确有对应，比如 Wynne and Rogers (2021) 记录了在任何搬迁之前，被驱逐的威胁就已经损害居民与地方的关系，这是一个本该在“搬迁”这一步才出现的后果，提前出现在了等待里。拓展：Rosengren 的第 3 步讲的是选择性的流动，谁会搬走，谁会留下。在 Waterloo，搬迁是业主决定的，而且去向在附近（Metro Quarter、Elizabeth Street），这是一种由国家安排的、本地的流动，和链条里默认的家庭自己的搬迁不同。我把它称作“被安排的流动”，这是我的概念，不是他们的。深化：药方的来源。Khor 等 (2023, 77) 引 Darcy and Rogers (2019)，把 70:30 追溯到郊区住宅区 Bonnyrigg，那里社会混合的结果“不是决策的决定因素”；Shrestha, Ruming and Rogers (2025) 说悉尼的更新模式“从外围，经中环，走向核心”。Claymore 的文件把问题归在 Radburn 式的设计上，说它造成“较差的舒适度”（NSW Land and Housing Corporation 2018）；Bonnyrigg 也是 Radburn 式的住宅区（Porter et al. 2023）。我读到的材料把这些来源住宅区描述成郊区的，而 Waterloo 是市中心的住宅区，建筑形式和位置都不同，所以这个药方在这里是否合适，是没有被检验的。

局限

理论预设和案例的错位

第一，理论预设弱势的聚集是原因。Sisson (2024) 指出，“集中的弱势”背后的邻里效应概念“有争议地”把原因放在住宅区上，而不是政治和经济原因上。居民的说法也不一致：居民 Julie 说“Waterloo 是我的家，我想留在这里”，活动人士 Karyn Brown 反驳部长说“它非常有功能”（Bowring 2026）。如果社区本身是资源，那么按链条把它读成待稀释的聚集，就读漏了一部分现实。第二，政策本身并不照理论走。如果政策完全按“稀释”的逻辑，就应该提高私人住房的比例；而 2023 年政府更替后，全区社会与可负担住房的比例是从 34% 提高到 50%（NSW Government 2023），并加入了回迁权。这说明政策同时在回应供给、居民和选举政治，而不是单纯的去集中。第三，理论没有考虑规则的层次：规划规则保证了楼面积，回迁只是声明（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 7, 14），所以“混合”这一步能被保证，后面的步骤只能靠承诺。这一点是本文自己的观察，理论里没有。这几条局限依据的是 2022 年的三份规划文件，再加 2025 至 2026 年的规划说明报告、评估要求和公示通知：这六份文件里，没有一份提到回迁（“return”一词检索为 0）。开发方的规划说明报告反而写道，更新“让现有社会住房居民长期不会被置换”，并“降低住房隔离”（Beam Planning 2025, PDF p. 15）：政策文件自己也在用隔离的语言，这说明五步链不只是我的读法，但这依然是声明，不是机制。环境影响报告、社会影响评估、公报版规则和 Homes NSW 的搬迁政策我没读到，如果它们把回迁写进了规则，“回迁只是声明”就要改。

写作规范

写进论文时，Rosengren 和 Tammaru 要标明据讲义；“被安排的流动”要标明是我的概念；“药方是否适合 Waterloo”只能写成“没有被检验”，不能写成“不适合”。

§5 绅士化

5. Gentrification: price, cost and promise

论文正文 Paper

5.1Tenants can lose their homes in two ways, and the promise of return covers only one of them. Gentrification research asks who gains when land is reinvested in and who bears the cost. Smith’s rent gap is the disparity between potential ground rent and the rent capitalised under the present use (Smith 1979), and Stein describes gentrification as “planned by the state, produced by the developers and consumed by the condo crowd” (Stein 2019, 41). Sydney’s council, arguing for more affordable housing at Green Square, says that gentrification “has exacerbated relative inequality among those who can and cannot afford housing” and that the market is becoming “virtually inaccessible to those on very low to moderate incomes” (City of Sydney n.d.). One way is through the market around an estate: Atkinson et al. (2011, 10) found for Sydney and Melbourne that displacement from gentrified neighbourhoods ran at about one and a half times the out-movement of similar households elsewhere, and that the most vulnerable displaced households were private renters, in data from the 2000s. The other way is direct: the estate’s own tenants are moved by their landlord, and the promise of return addresses only this second way. None of the documents I read addresses the first.

5.2Mixing is the policy that this remedy shares with “positive gentrification”, and the evidence for it is weak. Lees (2008) finds a “poor evidence base” for that policy, which is promoted in the belief that it will reduce segregation. Yrjänä (2026) shows in Helsinki how an anti-segregation policy can serve state-led gentrification, with a subsidy gap in which the state pays the difference to de-risk private investment. Hochstenbach (2017) finds for Amsterdam that the instruments of social mixing moved from the renewal of declining post-war neighbourhoods to high-demand neighbourhoods where housing policy pushes gentrification, the direction that Shrestha, Ruming and Rogers (2025) trace in Sydney. Davidson (2018, 249), reading new-build gentrification as large-scale capital working with state power, adds that the state “has been required to provide justifications for its actions”; for Waterloo the justification offered is the statewide diagnosis of section 4. Waterloo’s documents use the language of mixing, and Homes NSW states that “part of the success of mixed housing communities is that social and private housing are indistinguishable” (Homes NSW 2026). In the 2022 planning proposal, residential floor area on Land and Housing Corporation sites was split 26.5 per cent social, 7.0 per cent affordable and 66.5 per cent market (Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 19). The 2026 concept application proposes at least 30 per cent social housing and no more than 50 per cent market housing, with the balance affordable (NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026). Because the sale of private dwellings funds the project (section 3), I take the market share to be the part that pays for the rest, and that share has fallen from two thirds to at most a half as the policy changed. Whether this dilutes the cluster or prepares the area for richer residents, the documents do not show.

5.3On paper tenants are better protected than in 2015, but most of the protections are promises and not rules. They include the right to return, six months’ notice, staged relocation, more social homes and a share for Aboriginal households (NSW Government 2023, 2024; Homes NSW 2026). Of these, the right to return, notice and staging are not among the site-specific controls listed in the plan finalisation report (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 7). The reported gaps are also clear. The plan replaces 749 social dwellings with 847, an increase of 98 (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 8, 37; difference calculated), and under the previous government’s plan for the whole estate Sisson and Ruming (2025, 1839) calculated a net gain of 148, an uplift of 7 per cent. The minister could not say what part of the 20 per cent affordable share would be permanent (Bowring 2026), and activists said that at least ten people were still living on the site while the minister said that the remaining people were not tenants (Bowring 2026). No figures are published on how many tenants return or how soon. The question is therefore not whether the new buildings will exist but who will live in them and near them in ten years’ time. The documents promise return and do not show it.

框架

这一节用的是绅士化研究里的几个框架。Smith (1979) 的租金差：潜在地租与现有用途下被资本化的地租之间的差距。Stein (2019, 41)：绅士化由国家规划、由开发商生产、由公寓族消费（据讲义）。Lees (2008)：把绅士化当作促进社会混合的政策，证据基础薄弱。Yrjänä (2026)：赫尔辛基的反隔离政策服务于国家主导的绅士化，其中有补贴差距。Hochstenbach (2017)：阿姆斯特丹的社会混合手段，从衰败街区的更新，转向了政策在推动绅士化的高需求街区。Davidson (2018)：新建绅士化是大规模资本与国家权力的合作。

案例

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 对这些框架的价值，在于业主是国家，这让“谁获益”可以直接从计划里读出来。规划建议书里，土地与住房公司地块上的住宅建筑面积，社会住房占 26.5%，可负担住房占 7.0%，市场住房占 66.5%（Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 19）。因为私人住宅的销售资助着整个项目（Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208），我把市场那部分读成给其余部分出钱的部分。另外，Atkinson 等 (2011, 10) 的数据把置换分成了两条通道，案例把它们对上了号：周围市场里的私人租户（Atkinson 测的），和住宅区自己的租户被房东直接搬走（回迁承诺管的）。

赞同·反对·拓展

赞同、反对、拓展

赞同 Stein 和 Davidson：Waterloo 里国家确实是计划者，并且需要为自己的行动提供理由（Davidson 2018, 249），它提供的理由就是前面那个全州性的诊断。对 Lees 部分赞同：Waterloo 的混合，是把私人住宅放进一个社会住房占多数的地块，这符合她说的“正面的绅士化”，而她要求的证据，Waterloo 的文件里也没有。反对或限定 Yrjänä：在赫尔辛基，国家补上差额以降低私人投资的风险（Yrjänä 2026）；在 Waterloo，我读到的材料里资金流向相反，是私人住宅的销售在给社会住房出钱，更像交叉补贴而不是补贴。这个差别是一个限定：补贴差距的概念，在这里要换成别的东西。拓展 Hochstenbach：阿姆斯特丹的工具是出售现有的社会租赁住房，悉尼的工具是在公共土地上拆除重建，但方向相同，都是转向高需求的位置；Shrestha 等 (2025) 在悉尼勾勒的“从外围到核心”与此一致。

局限

国家不是铁板一块，政策也在变

这些框架常把国家写成单一的行动者，把绅士化写成阶级的重塑（Smith 在 Davidson 2018, 248 中的说法）。Waterloo 里国家是复数的：业主土地与住房公司，评估的规划部门，提了意见的市议会，计划定稿报告的附件里分别有对前两者的回应（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022）。而且结果被谈判改过：2023 年之后比例从 34% 提高到 50%，加入了回迁权、六个月通知和给原住民的份额（NSW Government 2023, 2024）。纯粹的“阶级重塑”解释，说不清这样的修订。另一个限定是，经典的置换研究（Atkinson 等 2011）测的是私人租户，数据来自 2000 年代，不能直接用在公屋租户的搬迁上；Waterloo 的更新让社会住房从 749 套增加到 847 套，所以“社会住房被拆掉就是置换”的简单说法，在这里也不完全适用，更关键的是谁回来、什么时候回来，而文件里没有这方面的数据。与此相对，Sisson and Ruming (2025, 1825) 发现他们考察的场地上社会住房套数减少，Waterloo 前一届政府的计划整个住宅区只净增 148 套、增幅 7%（同上, 1839）：相比之下 Waterloo 并不算差，但离“更新能大幅增加供给”的说法也很远。更直接的例子是市场住房的比例：它从 2022 年规划建议书里的 66.5%，降到 2026 年概念申请里的至多 50%（Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 19；NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026），说明国家主导的更新是在被一路谈判和修订，不能一次读成“阶级重塑”。这一节关于保护措施的局限，依据 2022 年的文件、2023 和 2024 年的公告和 2025 至 2026 年的概念申请材料；环境影响报告还没读，可能改变它。

§6 结论与讨论

6. Conclusion and discussion

论文正文 Paper

6.1The renewal mixes the site, and the planning rules guarantee only the mixing. Social housing falls from 86 to 28 per cent of the dwellings on the site, and the rules fix minimum floor areas for it, so step 1 of the chain is addressed. Whether the circle is broken or only moved depends on what the rules leave out: where the displaced tenants live, how many return, and what the market around the estate does. The evidence here shows some tenants placed in other social housing nearby and return promised to the rest, in a market where gentrification has widened the gap between households in private housing and in public housing estates (City of Sydney n.d.). The reputational gap that Zanardo et al. (2024) and Sisson (2024) describe may explain why the remedy is accepted. That is my suggestion, and it is the least tested part of the argument. On what the documents show, I cannot say that the circle is broken, and I cannot say that it is only moved: nothing in the planning documents I read would show which.

6.2This paper adds three small things to existing work on Waterloo. First, it reads the renewal through the chain of segregation. The studies of Waterloo that I used (Zanardo et al. 2024; Sisson 2024; Wynne and Rogers 2021; Sisson and Ruming 2025) deal with reputation, land value, displacement and housing supply, and none of them uses the vocabulary of segregation. Second, it sorts each protection for tenants by where it sits in the planning documents (Table 1) and finds that the rules guarantee floor area while return and relocation are statements. Third, it separates two things that are often run together: a plan can be described as dilution or as growth depending on whether it counts share or number, and tenants can lose their homes through the market around an estate or directly through their landlord, and only the second is covered by the promise of return. These are findings about documents. They do not test what happens to tenants.

6.3The case supports some frameworks and limits others. It is consistent with the view of Stein and Davidson that the state plans and justifies large-scale redevelopment (Davidson 2018, 249), and it extends the global-city account by showing that the pressure reached Waterloo through a metro station and not through the strategy that frames the corridor. It limits two accounts. In Yrjänä’s (2026) Helsinki the state pays a subsidy gap to de-risk private investment, while in the Waterloo documents I read the money runs the other way, with private sales paying for social homes. And accounts of home against real estate (Madden and Marcuse 2016), or of mixing against segregation, treat the two sides as opposed, while Waterloo’s policy has kept changing: the share of social and affordable housing rose from 34 to 50 per cent in 2023 (NSW Government 2023), the right to return was added, and a 2025 proposal would loosen how the Design Guide applies (NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2025a, 1). A single case and a set of documents can show such mechanisms. They cannot test the theories.

6.4This argument has limits. It rests on documents and published studies, not on interviews, so residents’ experience comes through other researchers and single news reports. I read three planning documents from 2022, the plan finalisation report, the final planning proposal and the Design Guide, and for the 2025–26 stage the proponent’s scoping report, the issued requirements and the exhibition notice. The environmental impact statement, the social impact assessment, the gazetted rules and the Homes NSW relocation policy I did not read, so “not among the rules” means only that a measure is not in the documents I read, and the finding may change if the exhibited documents write return into the rules. Two sources, Shrestha, Ruming and Rogers (2025) and Hochstenbach (2017), I read as abstracts. Destinations are named but not counted, and I found no counts of returning tenants and no measures of neighbourhood effects. The price series is for the whole city and turned in 2026, so the claim concerns two decades of rise, not the present. The five-step chain is my way of reading the case, and the documents do not use it. No official document explains the land-value funding model. The conclusion therefore holds for the 2022 documents and the 2025–26 summaries I read, and it may change when the exhibited proposal is read in full.

6.5Planning could do for people what it already does for floor space. The rules fix floor space by tenure; they could also require reporting on how many tenants return, when, and to the same place or only the same area, and where those who do not return live. The share of social housing should be published beside the number of social homes, because the number rises while the share falls. The permanence of the affordable homes should be confirmed. Where the existing buildings can be renovated, retention should be considered (Zanardo et al. 2024). None of this assumes bad faith. It asks that the cost of the remedy to the people it is meant to help be counted.

结论一节把前面四组理论放在一起。它们各自带着一个二分：家与房地产，混合与搬迁（隔离与绅士化），弱势聚集与稀释，国家与市场。理论的好处是把复杂的现实分成可以讨论的两端，代价是现实常常不在两端。

案例

案例加深了什么

把 Waterloo 的时间线摊开，每一个二分都变成了有层次、有时间的过程。家与房地产：同一个计划，用一个比例在不同年份里谈判。混合与搬迁：占比降了，套数增了，租户一部分被搬到附近的社会住房，其余的人被承诺回迁。弱势聚集与稀释：规则保证了稀释，但对回迁没有保证。国家与市场：国家既是业主、提案方，也是规划机关，社会住房的资金来自私人住宅的销售。所以，本文的结论“更新在场地上做到了混合，规则也只保证了混合”，实际上是在说：二分是对的，但它们在不同的层次上成立，一个层次上被保证的东西，另一个层次上只是承诺。

汇总

对各理论的汇总

赞同：Stein 和 Davidson 对国家角色的描述；Tammaru 的“不同领域相互关联”，通过 Wynne and Rogers 的“等待已造成伤害”得到支持；Lees 对社会混合证据薄弱的批评，Waterloo 的文件里确实没有结果证据。拓展：Sassen 的全球城市，补上“压力经由地铁站传导”；Rosengren 的第 3 步，补上“被安排的流动”；Atkinson 的置换，分出“周围市场”和“房东直接搬走”两条通道。深化：Madden and Marcuse，冲突发生在国家内部，也发生在对房屋状况的判断里；Shrestha 等的“从外围到核心”，补上药方的来源并非效果证据。限定：Yrjänä 的补贴差距，在 Waterloo 里更像交叉补贴；Aalbers 的金融化，我在这里用得宽松。

局限

这个案例能说和不能说什么

一个案例，只用文件，可以显示机制，不能检验理论。本文的发现只对 2022 年的计划定稿报告、最终规划建议书和设计指南成立；2025 年的评估小组报告说有一项提案要“提高建筑高度、重新分配楼面积，并对设计指南的适用引入灵活性”（NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2025a, 1），2026 年 5 至 6 月公示。我读了公示通知（它列出了修订：降低非住宅楼面积的最低要求、删除第 7.29(4) 条，以及社会住房至少 30% 的概念方案）、开发方的规划说明报告和发出的评估要求，没有读到提案条文本身和环境影响报告，所以“回迁只是声明”这个结论可能因此改变。这恰好是理论的另一个提醒：政策是持续在变的东西，用一份时点的文件去验证一个理论，容易把一个时点当成了全部。我没有租户一边的材料，没有回迁和去向的人数，也没有邻里效应的测量。

下一步

可以怎样继续

下一步最有价值的是三件事：读 2026 年的提案，看回迁是否写进了规则；找回迁和去向的数据；拿 Bonnyrigg、Airds 这类在更早阶段已经更新过的住宅区做对照，看“规则保证数量、回迁靠承诺”的结构是否也出现在那里。
