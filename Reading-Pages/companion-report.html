§2 全球城市

2. The global city: why Waterloo is drawn into the dispute

论文正文 Paper

2.1The strategy that frames Sydney as a global city does not name Waterloo. The state’s 2014 metropolitan strategy defines a Global Economic Corridor that “generates over 41 per cent of the NSW Gross State Product” and lists Green Square among its strategic centres, in line with the global-city idea that a city’s competitiveness rests on central land, infrastructure and the services of a global economy (Sassen 1991; NSW Planning and Environment 2014, 44). So the question for the renewal is what happens to the people already living on the land.

2.2The renewal is paid for by selling new private flats, and the price of those flats has risen strongly. The median transfer price of attached dwellings in Sydney rose from $390,000 in June 2006 to $840,000 in June 2026 (Australian Bureau of Statistics 2026; Figure 1). The documents examined give no price or rent figures for the Waterloo site itself.

2.3What brought the pressure to Waterloo was a metro station, which the urban design study calls “the catalyst for the renewal” (Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6) and the planning proposal cites for its “potential catalytic effects” (NSW Land and Housing Corporation [LAHC] 2020, 92). The reason given for replacing the housing is a different one. Future Directions, a statewide strategy, says that “many estates experience high levels of crime, unemployment…” (NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 24). This describes estates in general, not Waterloo, and section 4 tests it.

伴读报告 Companion

框架

这一节用的是全球城市的框架。按讲义里的说法，Sassen (1991) 认为全球化让生产分散、控制集中，少数城市因此成为管理和协调全球经济的枢纽，它们的竞争力靠中心区的土地、基础设施和高端服务；Brenner and Schmid 的星球城市化则提醒，不要把城市化只看成个别城市的事。这套框架回答的是一个城市尺度的问题：为什么一座城市需要更多、更贵的中心区土地。需要说明的是，这些作者我是据讲义引用的，没有读原文。

案例

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 把这个城市尺度的问题落到一块具体的土地上，并且显示了框架里看不见的一环：压力是怎样到达这块地的。2014 年的大都市战略划出“全球经济走廊”，把 Green Square 列为战略中心，文本里没有点名 Waterloo（NSW Planning and Environment 2014, 44）。而论证更新的文件，把直接的推动力说成地铁站：城市设计研究称车站是“这次更新的催化剂”（Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6），规划建议书说保持现状会忽视车站“潜在的催化效应”（LAHC 2020, 92）。所以，全球城市的压力到达 Waterloo，经过的是一项交通投资，而不是经过那份框定走廊的战略。这是对这些文件的归纳，不是文件自己的话。

赞同·拓展

赞同与拓展

对 Sassen 的方向，案例是赞同的：中心区土地确实被重新估价，悉尼附属住宅的中位转手价从 2006 年 6 月的 39 万澳元涨到 2026 年 6 月的 84 万澳元（Australian Bureau of Statistics 2026）。案例拓展的是机制：全球城市理论说“为什么需要这块地”，案例补上“压力经由什么落到这块地”，答案是基础设施。

局限

理论太线性，Waterloo 不是

框架暗含一个线性的预期：压力上升，重建随之推进。Waterloo 的时间线不是这样的。更新 2015 年宣布，规划路径 2019 年从州转到市议会，2021 年又回到州（Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6；NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 11）。现任部长自己在新闻稿里说，更新“宣布后停滞多年，直到 2022 年才有进展”（Scully 2026）。2026 年还出现了反向的信号：Cotality 的指数显示悉尼房价一年下降 9.4%（Cotality 2026），同年首批约 150 套住宅的围栏却开始搭建（Bowring 2026）。这说明落地的时间不是由价格单独决定的，政府更替和规划权的安排同样重要。需要注意，Scully 的新闻稿是执政方部长的发言，带着“我们 2023 年选举后把愿景变成行动”的叙事，所以我只把它当作官方对时间线的自述，不当作中立的历史。

小结

这一节里，全球城市框架解释了“为什么有压力”，案例补上了“压力经由地铁站传导”，并指出了框架不解释的部分：什么时候落地、由谁推动、以什么形式落地。写进论文时，措辞要用“与……一致”“补充了……”，不能写成“证明”或“推翻”Sassen：一个案例、一类文件，只能说明机制，不能检验理论。

§3 住房

3. Housing: the strategy, and the conflict between home and real estate

论文正文 Paper

3.1The plan has treated Waterloo both as people’s homes and as a way to pay for new housing. In December 2015 the state announced that the estate would be demolished and rebuilt with about 30 per cent social and 70 per cent private housing, the sale of the private dwellings funding the project (Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208), in line with a statewide target of 70:30 (NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 9). The planning proposal says the existing properties are “at the end of their economic life” (LAHC 2020, 23), yet the same study gives their average age as 44 years against a benchmark life of 60 (Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6). Madden and Marcuse (2016) call this tension a conflict between home and real estate: housing as lived, social space against housing as an instrument for profit-making.

3.2The promises to tenants grew after 2023, but the planning rules were written before them (Figure 2). The plan approved in 2022 provides 3,012 dwellings, of which 847 are social housing, and its planning controls date from November 2022 (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 37). After a change of government in March 2023, the share of social and affordable housing across the estate was raised from 34 to 50 per cent, tenants were promised the right to return, and relocation was to proceed in small stages (NSW Government 2023); in 2024 it added at least six months’ notice and dedicated support (NSW Government 2024). By 2026 a new concept application proposed about 3,300 homes, at least half of them social or affordable (Scully 2026). So the promises moved ahead of the rules. The floor-area part has since reached the planning documents: the proponent’s 2025 scoping report describes 50 per cent of residential floor area as committed to social and affordable housing (Beam Planning 2025, PDF pp. 4, 20), and the 2026 concept application proposes at least 30 per cent of residential floor area as social housing and no more than 50 per cent as market housing (NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026). None of the three documents for this stage mentions a right of return.

3.3The strategy can be sorted into measures that protect the money and measures that protect the tenants’ homes. On the money side, the planning proposal says that the existing planning rules “would not be capable of supporting a feasible redevelopment of the entire site given the costs of demolition and construction” (LAHC 2020, 92), and the sale of private dwellings carries the cost. On the tenants’ side are the right to return and more social homes in Waterloo South than there are now (Homes NSW 2026a). No official document sorts the strategy in this way. Sections 4 and 5 ask which side the binding rules protect.

伴读报告 Companion

框架

这一节的框架是住房研究里“家与房地产”的对立。Madden and Marcuse (2016, 4) 把今天的住房描述为：住房作为生活的、社会性的空间，与住房作为盈利的工具，两者之间有冲突，也就是家与房地产之间的冲突（据讲义）。讲义里还有 Aalbers (2016) 的住房金融化：金融动机、金融市场和金融行为者在塑造住房上的作用越来越大，而且结果因情境而异。

案例

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 让这个对立变得具体：同一个计划同时承载两边。一边是房地产：规划建议书说现有规划“不足以支持对整个场地进行可行的重新开发”（LAHC 2020, 92），私人住宅的销售资助整个项目（Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208）。另一边是家：回迁权、比现在更多的 Waterloo South 社会住房（Homes NSW 2026）。更有意思的是，两边是通过一个比例在谈判：2015 年约 30% 社会住房、70% 私人住房（Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208）；一份早期提案只有在 30% 的住宅建筑面积是社会住房、20% 是可负担住房时才给额外楼面积（NSW Land and Housing Corporation 2021, Attachment A）；2022 年定稿的控制是社会住房 26.5%、可负担住房 7.0%（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 7）；2023 年政府更替后，全区社会与可负担住房的比例从 34% 提高到 50%（NSW Government 2023）。对立在这里表现为一个数字在多年里被反复改写。

拓展·深化

拓展与深化

第一，拓展：Madden and Marcuse 的对立通常被读成租户与开发商之间的冲突，Waterloo 显示冲突也发生在国家内部。提出计划的是土地与住房公司，评估它的是规划部门，市议会也提了意见，计划定稿报告的附件列表里有对土地与住房公司和对市议会的分别回应（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, 附件目录）。比例的谈判在报告里留有痕迹：有一条建议要求至少恢复公示版规划建议书里“土地与住房公司地块上至少 30% 的住宅楼面积是社会住房、20% 是可负担住房”，答复是保留 26.5% 和 7.0%，理由是这些最低值与 Gateway 决定一致，后者要求至少 847 套社会住房（同上，PDF p. 30）。2021 年 3 月规划权又收回到州（同上，PDF p. 11），所以同一个国家既是业主，又是提案方，也是规划机关。第二，深化：房屋状况本身成了争论的材料。规划建议书说房产“已到经济寿命的尽头”（LAHC 2020, 23），而同一份研究给出的平均房龄是 44 年，基准寿命是 60 年（Turner Studio & Turf 2020, Part 1, PDF p. 6）；Zanardo 等 (2024) 则认为，除板楼可能是例外外，大多数建筑只需相对适度的翻新就能延长寿命。也就是说，“家”这一边也要靠对建筑状况的判断来论证。

局限

对立不是非黑即白

理论把两边写成对立，案例显示的是混合：保护租户的“家”，在这里要靠出售房地产才能出钱。Homes NSW 说新的 Waterloo South 会有比现在更多的社会住房（Homes NSW 2026），按计划的数字是 749 套增加到 847 套，只多 98 套（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 8, 37；差值由计算得出），而占比却从约 86% 降到约 28%。同一个计划，按套数数是增长，按占比数是稀释，所以“房地产逻辑赢了还是家赢了”取决于你用哪个分母，理论本身没有给出判断的标准。另外，Aalbers 的金融化概念通常指机构投资者、上市的房地产信托之类。Waterloo 的文件里，资金来自出售私人住宅，没有看到机构投资者，所以论文 §4.3 用“金融化”一词是宽松的用法，不是 Aalbers 本人的结论。比例也在继续变：2026 年的概念申请提出社会住房至少占住宅建筑面积的 30%、市场住房至多 50%（NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026），开发方 2025 年的规划说明报告把住宅建筑面积的 50% 写成已承诺给社会和可负担住房（Beam Planning 2025, PDF pp. 4, 20）。所以“房地产逻辑赢了还是家赢了”不是一次定下的结果，而是被一路改写的数字。这里的判断依据所分析的文件；环境影响报告正文和公报版规则没有读，如果它们改了比例或把回迁写进规则，这里要跟着改。

写作规范

在论文里，这一节只能说：案例“与”家与房地产的对立“一致”，并“显示”这个对立由比例和分母来承载；“冲突在国家内部”是基于文件分工做的推论，要写成推论。

§4 隔离

4. Segregation: testing the remedy against the chain

论文正文 Paper

4.1The state’s case for renewal rests on an argument that segregation research also makes: disadvantage that clusters in one place reproduces itself. Tammaru et al. (2021) describe vicious circles of segregation as the product of connected social and spatial inequalities in different life domains: advantage breeds advantage and disadvantage breeds disadvantage. Five steps can be distinguished (Figure 3): disadvantage clusters spatially, neighbourhood effects follow, poor perceptions shape who moves, segregation spreads to other domains such as schools and work, and it becomes immobility across the life course and across generations (Rosengren 2026). Future Directions follows the same chain. It finds disadvantage concentrated in estates, says that its effects “can be passed on through multiple generations, reinforcing the cycle of disadvantage” (NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 24), and proposes mixed tenure, with a 70:30 split between private and social dwellings “to enable more integrated communities” (PDF p. 9).

4.2On step 1 the plan works: social housing falls from 86 per cent of the site’s dwellings to 28 per cent. Waterloo South now has 749 social and 120 private dwellings, and the plan approved in 2022 has 847 social dwellings among 3,012 (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 8, 37; shares calculated from these figures; Figure 4). Homes NSW now gives about 1,050 social homes among about 3,300 (Homes NSW 2026a), about 32 per cent. This is dilution by social mixing, which makes concentrated disadvantage less visible. The same plan can also be described as growth, 749 social dwellings becoming about 1,050, so a document can call it dilution or growth depending on whether it counts share or number.

4.2bWho lives on the site shapes what step 1 dilutes. In February 2026, 607 tenant households with 836 residents remained on Waterloo South: 77 per cent were single-person households, 62 per cent had a tenant over 55 (or over 45 for Aboriginal tenants), and 19 per cent of tenants were Aboriginal (Homes NSW 2026b, PDF p. 7). In September 2019 their households had an average income of $525 a week, 73 per cent lived alone and 33 per cent drew their main income from a disability pension (GHD 2020, PDF pp. 30, 34). The poverty line for a single adult in 2019–20 was $489 a week (Davidson, Bradbury and Wong 2023, PDF p. 8), so the average household was near it. Nationally 25 per cent of public housing households receive a disability pension (Porter et al. 2023, 21), so Waterloo is higher but not different in kind. Its neighbourhoods ranked in the two lowest deciles of disadvantage in the state (GHD 2020, PDF p. 30). The concentration is therefore one of low income, age, disability and living alone. Steps 4 and 5 of the chain concern schools, work and the next generation, and 149 of the 995 tenants in July 2025 were under 18 (Homes NSW 2025a, PDF p. 19).

4.2cThe area around the estate has already been mixed by the market. The social baseline report says that the surroundings “have undergone significant redevelopment in recent years”, which “changed local demographics, with more tertiary students, … professionals, young families, couples without children” (GHD 2020, PDF p. 3). Ten of the 15 neighbourhood units of Waterloo East rank in the top three deciles of advantage, though three rank in the lowest, and the suburb as a whole ranks in the second decile (PDF pp. 37, 75). The report expects the renewal of Waterloo South to be “highly likely to attract a similar demographic” (PDF p. 31). The median household income of the suburb rose from $1,503 a week in the 2016 census to $2,028 in 2021, a nominal rise of about 35 per cent (GHD 2020, PDF p. 35; Australian Bureau of Statistics 2021). That figure covers private households as well as tenants and does not describe the estate.

4.2dThe chain also runs at the scale of the whole city, and the remedy acts on the estate. In Greater Sydney the large social-housing estates that dominate a disadvantaged suburb lie mainly in the western suburbs: of 177 disadvantaged suburbs in Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane, 14 are dominated by social housing and 13 of those are in Sydney (Hulse et al. 2014, PDF pp. 16, 40). Waterloo is not one of them. The same study had to set aside Waterloo and Haymarket as statistical outliers, Waterloo for “rapid gentrification” (PDF p. 38). The latest metropolitan study finds Sydney’s segregation rising because very-high-income households cluster in the least disadvantaged areas “rather than” because very-low-income households cluster (Sarkar et al. 2024, PDF p. 34). The state’s metropolitan plan lists “areas of high social housing concentration where there is good access to services, transport and jobs” as candidates for renewal (Greater Sydney Commission 2018, PDF p. 61), which points renewal at well-served locations such as Waterloo’s. Pawson, Hulse and Cheshire (2015, PDF p. 12) call the use of local solutions for systemic causes a “scalar mismatch”. Renewing Waterloo can therefore reduce concentration on the estate without changing the metropolitan pattern. No study that was found follows where inner-city tenants go across Sydney; the one statement available, for Millers Point, is that most were settled within 10 km of the city centre (Morris 2017, PDF p. 7).

4.2eTaken together, these facts change what step 1 achieves. The estate is a pocket of low-income, older and disabled households inside a neighbourhood the market has already mixed, and Sydney’s concentrations of disadvantage lie elsewhere. Adding private housing to the estate mixes at the scale of the block what the neighbourhood already mixes at the scale of the street, so the mix cannot be the measure of success. What the estate’s residents stand to keep or lose is their place, and the test that the chain implies is retention: whether the households now there remain.

4.3The 70:30 remedy was not designed from evidence about what mixing achieves. Khor et al. (2023, 77), citing Darcy and Rogers (2019), trace the ratio to Bonnyrigg, a suburban estate, where social-mixing outcomes “was not a determinant of decision-making”. None of these sources counts returning tenants or measures neighbourhood effects. That outcomes were not a determinant fits the financialisation of housing (Aalbers 2016), in which housing is treated as an asset, and the rules then enforce a quantity of tenure and not an outcome.

4.4Where displaced tenants go decides whether the cluster is dissolved or only moved. By November 2025 about 60 tenants of Stage 1 had been relocated, “either to a home in the local area or to another area of their choice in NSW” (Homes NSW 2025b, PDF p. 3), and in mid-2026 the ABC reported that at least 70 current tenants had moved into the new homes beside the Waterloo metro station (Bowring 2026). Homes NSW names two local destinations, the 70 social homes at the Metro Quarter and about 95 more at Elizabeth Street, Redfern (Homes NSW 2026a). The counts differ in date and source, and no document gives the number of tenants at each destination. What can be said is that the plan dilutes social housing on the site while also placing social housing nearby.

4.5The planning rules guarantee how much social housing is built, but not who lives in it and not whether tenants can come back. The site-specific controls that the plan finalisation report lists for Waterloo South require no less than 26.5 per cent of residential floor area as social housing, no less than 7.0 per cent as affordable housing, and at least 5,000 square metres for community, health and childcare facilities (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 7). These are minimum amounts of floor area. Such minimums fix which kinds of housing the new buildings contain, not which households hold them. The Design Guide, where the tenure and Aboriginal housing provisions sit, is only to be considered in development applications “but not to elevate the guidance within the design guide to development standards” (PDF p. 7).

4.6Return and relocation appear as guidance and statements, not as planning controls. The final planning proposal records that the Land and Housing Corporation “has noted” that current residents have a right of return (Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 59), and the department accepted a response that “appropriate relocation strategies will be developed” (NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF p. 14). Neither document requires anyone to track what happens to tenants after they move. The strategy has since been written down. The support guide of February 2026 gives tenants at least six months’ notice and up to two offers of housing, check-ins soon after a move and about three and six months later, and the right for “all existing Waterloo tenants at the time of relocations” to return once new buildings are complete, which “will be an individual choice for each tenant” (Homes NSW 2026b, PDF pp. 14, 16). The guide is not a planning control and sets no deadline for return, and none of the four Homes NSW relocation documents says what happens to the right if a tenant dies or enters aged care before the buildings are finished. The body of the impact statement, the social impact assessment (its Appendix EE) and the gazetted rules are outside this reading (section 6.4). In terms of the five steps, the rules reach step 1; steps 3 and 5, which concern where people go and what happens to them, depend on guidance and promises (Table 1).

4.6bWhere return has been counted elsewhere, it has been low. About 40 per cent of residents came back to Carlton in Melbourne, after being moved first to other estates or to dwellings elsewhere, and 20 per cent to Kensington (Porter et al. 2023, 30, citing Levin et al. 2018 and Shaw et al. 2013). In Chicago, fewer than 11 per cent of the 16,846 relocated family households lived in the new mixed-income developments at the end of 2010 (Joseph and Chaskin 2012, 378), and the average return rate at four HOPE VI sites was 5 per cent (Comey 2007, cited in Joseph and Chaskin 2012, 379). Goetz (2010) ties low return to the years between displacement and completion. Bonnyrigg in Sydney is the closest model, since staged building let tenants move within the estate, and Porter et al. (2023, 30) expect Waterloo to follow it. Bonnyrigg still lost about 200 social dwellings (Porter et al. 2023, 30), whereas the Waterloo South plan raises the number from 749 to about 1,050. These are other cities and other programmes, and none gives a Waterloo figure.

4.7Steps 2 to 5 are where the evidence is thinnest. Sisson (2024) notes that the concept of neighbourhood effects behind “concentrated disadvantage” “controversially” places the cause in the estate. Reviews offer limited evidence on steps 2 to 5: a systematic review of 27 UK studies found some positive effects of mixed tenure in the social and residential domains but none on social capital (Sautkina, Bond and Kearns 2012), and Manley, van Ham and Doherty (2011) conclude that the evidence base for social mixing is “far from robust”. Uncertainty already acts on steps three and five. Wynne and Rogers (2021) argue that the threat of eviction “has already done significant damage to their relationship to place” before any move, and a resident, Julie, said that she heard of the demolition work when she woke to find fences going up, and that her relocation had been delayed (Bowring 2026). No document measures residents’ mobility or opportunities.

伴读报告 Companion

框架

这一节用的是隔离研究的框架。Tammaru 等 (2021) 把隔离的恶性循环描述为不同生活领域里社会不平等与空间不平等相互关联的产物：优势滋生优势，弱势滋生弱势。讲义里 Rosengren (2026) 列出五个步骤：弱势在空间上聚集，邻里效应随之而来，不佳的观感影响谁会搬迁，隔离扩展到学校、工作等其他领域，最终成为跨越代际的不能流动。这两处我是据讲义引用的。

案例

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 先显示的是：这条链不只是学者的说法，国家自己用它来论证更新。Future Directions 说弱势集中在住宅区里，其影响“可以代代相传，强化弱势的循环”（NSW Government 2016, PDF p. 24），药方是 70:30 的混合租住（同上，PDF p. 9）。其次，案例让每一步的检验变得具体：第 1 步，社会住房在场地住宅中的占比从约 86% 降到约 28%（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 8, 37）；第 3 步，租户被搬到哪里，点名的去向是 Metro Quarter 的 70 套和 Redfern Elizabeth Street 约 95 套（Homes NSW 2026）。第 2、4、5 步，所分析的文件没有测量。

赞同、拓展与深化

赞同：Tammaru 的“不同领域相互关联”在 Waterloo 里确有对应，比如 Wynne and Rogers (2021) 记录了在任何搬迁之前，被驱逐的威胁就已经损害居民与地方的关系，这是一个本该在“搬迁”这一步才出现的后果，提前出现在了等待里。拓展：Rosengren 的第 3 步讲的是选择性的流动，谁会搬走，谁会留下。在 Waterloo，搬迁是业主决定的，而且去向在附近（Metro Quarter、Elizabeth Street），这是一种由国家安排的、本地的流动，和链条里默认的家庭自己的搬迁不同。论文把它称作“被安排的流动”，这是本文的概念，不是他们的。深化：药方的来源。Khor 等 (2023, 77) 引 Darcy and Rogers (2019)，把 70:30 追溯到郊区住宅区 Bonnyrigg，那里社会混合的结果“不是决策的决定因素”；Shrestha, Ruming and Rogers (2025) 说悉尼的更新模式“从外围，经中环，走向核心”。Claymore 的文件把问题归在 Radburn 式的设计上，说它造成“较差的舒适度”（NSW Land and Housing Corporation 2018）；Bonnyrigg 也是 Radburn 式的住宅区（Porter et al. 2023）。所分析的材料把这些来源住宅区描述成郊区的，而 Waterloo 是市中心的住宅区，建筑形式和位置都不同，所以这个药方在这里是否合适，是没有被检验的。

局限

理论预设和案例的错位

第一，理论预设弱势的聚集是原因。Sisson (2024) 指出，“集中的弱势”背后的邻里效应概念“有争议地”把原因放在住宅区上，而不是政治和经济原因上。居民的说法也不一致：居民 Julie 说“Waterloo 是我的家，我想留在这里”，活动人士 Karyn Brown 反驳部长说“它非常有功能”（Bowring 2026）。如果社区本身是资源，那么按链条把它读成待稀释的聚集，就读漏了一部分现实。另一处错位在人口上：Waterloo South 的住户里，68% 的户主超过 50 岁，73% 独居，33% 的主要收入来自残疾抚恤金（GHD 2020, PDF pp. 30, 34），而链条后几步讲的学校、工作和下一代，只涉及其中不到三分之一的、户主在 50 岁以下的住户。理论里的“集中的弱势”，在这里首先是年龄和残疾。第二，政策本身并不照理论走。如果政策完全按“稀释”的逻辑，就应该提高私人住房的比例；而 2023 年政府更替后，全区社会与可负担住房的比例是从 34% 提高到 50%（NSW Government 2023），并加入了回迁权。这说明政策同时在回应供给、居民和选举政治，而不是单纯的去集中。第三，理论没有考虑规则的层次：规划规则保证了楼面积，回迁只是声明（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022, PDF pp. 7, 14），所以“混合”这一步能被保证，后面的步骤只能靠承诺。这一点是本文自己的观察，理论里没有。这几条局限依据的是 2022 年的三份规划文件，再加 2025 至 2026 年的规划说明报告、评估要求和公示通知：这六份文件里，没有一份提到回迁（“return”一词检索为 0）。开发方的规划说明报告反而写道，更新“让现有社会住房居民长期不会被置换”，并“降低住房隔离”（Beam Planning 2025, PDF p. 15）：政策文件自己也在用隔离的语言，这说明五步链不只是本文套用的框架，但这依然是声明，不是机制。环境影响报告正文、社会影响评估、公报版规则和 Homes NSW 的搬迁政策没有读（环境影响报告只读了附录 A 至 E），如果它们把回迁写进了规则，“回迁只是声明”就要改。

§5 绅士化

5. Gentrification: price, cost and promise

论文正文 Paper

5.1The renewal is a case of state-led, new-build gentrification. Davidson and Lees (2005, 1170) give four defining elements: reinvestment of capital, social upgrading of the locale by incoming high-income groups, landscape change, and direct or indirect displacement of low-income groups. The first three are present at Waterloo. The sale of private dwellings reinvests capital in public land and closes Smith’s (1979) rent gap, between potential and actual ground rent, through the state itself; the landlord’s consultants expect the renewal to attract “a similar demographic” to Waterloo East (GHD 2020, PDF p. 31); and the buildings are replaced. The fourth is the question of this section. Hackworth and Smith (2001, 468–469) describe a third wave in which the state is “more involved in the process” and large developers orchestrate reinvestment. The surrounding area has been rebuilt, so the estate is renewed late in the process, not at its start, and Hulse et al. (2014, PDF p. 38) already treat Waterloo as an outlier for “rapid gentrification” in 2001–2011. No published author places Waterloo on a named stage, so this placement is the paper’s own, and it rests on the features listed.

5.2Mixing is the policy that this remedy shares with “positive gentrification”, and the evidence for it is weak. Lees (2008) finds a “poor evidence base” for that policy, which is promoted in the belief that it will reduce segregation. Yrjänä (2026) shows in Helsinki how an anti-segregation policy can serve state-led gentrification, and Hochstenbach (2017) finds for Amsterdam that the instruments of social mixing moved to high-demand neighbourhoods, the direction that Shrestha, Ruming and Rogers (2025) trace in Sydney. Davidson (2008, 2402) puts the question that mixing leaves open: “how many people should be displaced—lose their home and neighbourhood—before it becomes a problem?” He adds that social housing tenants “may be insulated from some indirect displacement pressures” but not others (2008, 2391). There are two routes to better conditions for the people on the estate: upgrade the buildings for them where they are, or sell part of the land to richer residents to pay for new ones. The documents take the second. The market share, which pays for the rest, fell from 66.5 per cent of residential floor area in the 2022 proposal to at most 50 per cent in the 2026 concept (Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 19; NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026).

5.3Marcuse (1985, 205–208) separates four forms of displacement and says that all four must be counted. Direct displacement moves households whose homes become unusable or unavailable through conditions beyond their control, chain displacement counts those who left a unit before its last resident, exclusionary displacement keeps similar households from moving in, and displacement pressure is the strain of watching the neighbourhood change around you. At Waterloo the first is the landlord’s own act. The plan guards it with notice, two offers and a right to return, but only as an individual choice for existing tenants (Homes NSW 2026b, PDF p. 16). The rebuild takes 10 to 15 years (Homes NSW 2026c, PDF p. 2), and 62 per cent of households have a tenant over 55 (45 for Aboriginal tenants), so the right to return competes with time. In NSW, 15 per cent of 70-year-old women and 23 per cent of 70-year-old men die within ten years, and 31 and 43 per cent within fifteen (Australian Bureau of Statistics 2024, NSW life table 2021–23, calculated from the survivors column). These are general-population rates, and the Chicago panel of public housing tenants had 14 per cent dead after eight years, about twice the general rate (Price and Popkin 2010, 4). The sums are illustrative, because Waterloo’s tenants’ ages are not published in bands, but they show that return depends on the length of the wait as well as on willingness. In Melbourne about 40 per cent returned to Carlton and 20 per cent to Kensington (Porter et al. 2023, 30).

5.3bThe other three forms are guarded unevenly. Chain displacement follows from relocation in phases over the same 10 to 15 years, with about 60 Stage 1 tenants gone by November 2025 (Homes NSW 2025b, PDF p. 3). Exclusionary displacement is the form the plan guards best: the number of social homes rises from 749 to about 1,050, and at least 7 per cent of residential floor area is to be affordable housing held in perpetuity by a community housing provider, with other affordable housing kept for at least 25 years, which the applicant expects to be imposed as consent conditions (Beam Planning 2026, App. E, PDF p. 6). The share of social housing nonetheless falls from 86 to about 32 per cent. Displacement pressure is not guarded: the market around the estate has already changed it, and Atkinson et al. (2011, PDF p. 10) found that in gentrified areas of Sydney and Melbourne the rate at which public renters moved out was higher than expected and higher than elsewhere, a change they link to “estate remodelling and demolitions”. None of the planning documents examined addresses it.

5.4Marcuse (1984–85, 933–934) observes that the costs and benefits of gentrification fall on different classes, and that public resources invested in a neighbourhood “will not help existing lower-income residents without specific provisions designed to achieve that end”. At Waterloo the cost of the move falls on low-income, mostly older tenants, and the benefits go to the buyers of the new flats, to the state that sells the land, and to the further social homes that the sales pay for. Counting the four forms, the plan guards the number of social homes, gives tenants a conditional route back, and leaves chain displacement and displacement pressure to time and the market. Freeman (2005) finds that gentrifying neighbourhoods in New York could change without much displacement, but his data do not isolate a landlord relocating its own tenants for a rebuild. The question is therefore not whether the new buildings will exist but whether the people who live there now will be among those who live in them. The documents promise return and do not show it.

类型、阶段与四种置换

Davidson and Lees (2005, 1170) 给出当代绅士化的四个要素：资本的再投入、高收入者迁入带来的社会升级、景观的改变，以及对低收入者的直接或间接置换。Waterloo 的前三项很明显，第四项是争议所在。Hackworth and Smith (2001, 468–469) 把绅士化分成几波，第三波的特点是国家“比以前更深地参与”、大开发商来组织再投入；Waterloo 的做法（国家出地、卖房出钱）符合这些特点。但没有已发表的作者把 Waterloo 放在某个阶段，Hulse 等 (2014) 只在统计上把它标为“快速绅士化”的异常值，所以“后段、国家主导、新建式”是本文自己的判断，依据要列出来。衡量公平，Marcuse (1985, 205–208) 的四种置换最有用：直接置换、连锁置换、排他性置换和置换压力，而且四种都要算，只算一种会低估。Davidson (2008) 补充说，社会住房租户可能不受某些间接置换压力的影响，但不是全部，所以“还住在原地”不等于没有被挤压。Waterloo 的保护并不均衡：排他性置换保护得最好（社会住房从 749 套增加到约 1,050 套）；直接置换只有个人选择的回迁权，而重建要用 10 到 15 年，62% 的户里有 55 岁以上的租户，时间本身就会让一部分人用不上这项权利；置换压力没有任何规划文件处理。Freeman (2005) 认为绅士化的邻里可以没有大量置换，但他的数据看不出房东为重建而搬走自己的租户，所以这个反方观点不能直接用在 Waterloo 上。

框架

这一节用的是绅士化研究里的几个框架。Smith (1979) 的租金差：潜在地租与现有用途下被资本化的地租之间的差距。Stein (2019, 41)：绅士化由国家规划、由开发商生产、由公寓族消费（据讲义）。Lees (2008)：把绅士化当作促进社会混合的政策，证据基础薄弱。Yrjänä (2026)：赫尔辛基的反隔离政策服务于国家主导的绅士化，其中有补贴差距。Hochstenbach (2017)：阿姆斯特丹的社会混合手段，从衰败街区的更新，转向了政策在推动绅士化的高需求街区。Davidson (2018)：新建绅士化是大规模资本与国家权力的合作。

案例加深了什么

Waterloo 对这些框架的价值，在于业主是国家，这让“谁获益”可以直接从计划里读出来。规划建议书里，土地与住房公司地块上的住宅建筑面积，社会住房占 26.5%，可负担住房占 7.0%，市场住房占 66.5%（Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 19）。因为私人住宅的销售资助着整个项目（Zanardo et al. 2024, 1208），我把市场那部分读成给其余部分出钱的部分。另外，Atkinson 等 (2011, 10) 的数据把置换分成了两条通道，案例把它们对上了号：周围市场里的私人租户（Atkinson 测的），和住宅区自己的租户被房东直接搬走（回迁承诺管的）。

赞同·反对·拓展

赞同、反对、拓展

赞同 Stein 和 Davidson：Waterloo 里国家确实是计划者，并且需要为自己的行动提供理由（Davidson 2018, 249），它提供的理由就是前面那个全州性的诊断。对 Lees 部分赞同：Waterloo 的混合，是把私人住宅放进一个社会住房占多数的地块，这符合她说的“正面的绅士化”，而她要求的证据，Waterloo 的文件里也没有。反对或限定 Yrjänä：在赫尔辛基，国家补上差额以降低私人投资的风险（Yrjänä 2026）；在 Waterloo，所分析的材料里资金流向相反，是私人住宅的销售在给社会住房出钱，更像交叉补贴而不是补贴。这个差别是一个限定：补贴差距的概念，在这里要换成别的东西。拓展 Hochstenbach：阿姆斯特丹的工具是出售现有的社会租赁住房，悉尼的工具是在公共土地上拆除重建，但方向相同，都是转向高需求的位置；Shrestha 等 (2025) 在悉尼勾勒的“从外围到核心”与此一致。

局限

国家不是铁板一块，政策也在变

这些框架常把国家写成单一的行动者，把绅士化写成阶级的重塑（Smith 在 Davidson 2018, 248 中的说法）。Waterloo 里国家是复数的：业主土地与住房公司，评估的规划部门，提了意见的市议会，计划定稿报告的附件里分别有对前两者的回应（NSW Department of Planning and Environment 2022）。而且结果被谈判改过：2023 年之后比例从 34% 提高到 50%，加入了回迁权、六个月通知和给原住民的份额（NSW Government 2023, 2024）。纯粹的“阶级重塑”解释，说不清这样的修订。另一个限定是，经典的置换研究（Atkinson 等 2011）测的是私人租户，数据来自 2000 年代，不能直接用在公屋租户的搬迁上；Waterloo 的更新让社会住房从 749 套增加到 847 套，所以“社会住房被拆掉就是置换”的简单说法，在这里也不完全适用，更关键的是谁回来、什么时候回来，而文件里没有这方面的数据。与此相对，Sisson and Ruming (2025, 1825) 发现他们考察的场地上社会住房套数减少，Waterloo 前一届政府的计划整个住宅区只净增 148 套、增幅 7%（同上, 1839）：相比之下 Waterloo 并不算差，但离“更新能大幅增加供给”的说法也很远。更直接的例子是市场住房的比例：它从 2022 年规划建议书里的 66.5%，降到 2026 年概念申请里的至多 50%（Hassell 2022a, PDF p. 19；NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2026），说明国家主导的更新是在被一路谈判和修订，不能一次读成“阶级重塑”。这一节关于保护措施的局限，依据 2022 年的文件、2023 和 2024 年的公告和 2025 至 2026 年的概念申请材料；环境影响报告还没读，可能改变它。

§6 结论与讨论

6. Conclusion and discussion

论文正文 Paper

6.1The approved plan dilutes the estate, and the planning documents secure that and not the people who live there. Social housing falls from 86 to 28 per cent of the dwellings on the site in the 2022 plan, and binding minimum floor areas guarantee that step 1 of the chain is addressed. Judged by the number of social homes, the safeguards are strong: 749 become about 1,050. Judged by the retention of the households now on the site, they are thin. Return is an individual choice for existing tenants, no document sets a deadline or a count, none says what happens if a tenant dies or enters aged care before the buildings are finished, and where return has been counted elsewhere between 5 and about 40 per cent of tenants came back. In a neighbourhood the market has already mixed, and in a metropolis where disadvantage lies elsewhere, adding richer neighbours does not by itself break the circle at Waterloo. Whether the circle is broken or only moved is decided by who is still there when the buildings are finished, and return cannot yet be observed, so this conclusion concerns the safeguards in the documents examined.

6.2This paper adds three small things to existing work on Waterloo. First, it reads the renewal as state-led, new-build gentrification and applies Marcuse’s four forms of displacement to it. Four studies of Waterloo (Zanardo et al. 2024; Sisson 2024; Wynne and Rogers 2021; Sisson and Ruming 2025) deal with reputation, land value, displacement and housing supply, and none uses the vocabulary of segregation or sets the plan against all four forms. Second, it sorts each protection for tenants by where it sits (Table 1) and finds that the rules guarantee floor area while return and relocation are guidance and statements. Third, it sets the design of the plan against age and time: a rebuild of 10 to 15 years, households in which 62 per cent have a tenant over 55, and return rates of 5 to 40 per cent elsewhere. It also separates two things often run together: the share and the number of social homes.

6.3The case supports some frameworks and limits others. It is consistent with the view of Stein and Davidson that the state plans and justifies large-scale redevelopment (Davidson 2018, 249), and it extends the global-city account by showing that the pressure reached Waterloo through a metro station and not through the strategy that frames the corridor. It limits two accounts. In Yrjänä’s (2026) Helsinki the state pays a subsidy gap to de-risk private investment, while in the Waterloo documents the money runs the other way, with private sales paying for social homes. And accounts of home against real estate (Madden and Marcuse 2016), or of mixing against segregation, treat the two sides as opposed, while Waterloo’s policy kept changing: the share of social and affordable housing rose from 34 to 50 per cent in 2023 (NSW Government 2023), and the right to return was added.

6.4This argument has limits. It rests on documents and published studies, not on interviews, so residents’ experience comes through other researchers and single news reports. Return cannot yet be observed, because the first new buildings are not finished, so the paper judges safeguards and not outcomes. The body of the environmental impact statement, the social impact assessment, the gazetted rules and the Homes NSW relocation policy beyond its guides were not read (of the impact statement only appendices A to E were), so “not among the rules” means only that a measure is not in the documents examined. The survival figures are for the general population of NSW and are illustrative, because the tenants’ ages are not published in bands. Shrestha, Ruming and Rogers (2025) and Hochstenbach (2017) are known here from their abstracts. Destinations are named but not counted, and the figures on return come from other cities, where tenure, programme and period differ. The documents do not use the five-step chain or Marcuse’s four forms; they are the frames applied to them, and no study applying the chain to Sydney, or following inner-city tenants’ destinations across the metropolitan area, was found. The placement of Waterloo as late-stage, state-led, new-build gentrification is the paper’s own. The conclusion therefore holds for the documents examined and may change when the exhibited proposal is read in full.

6.5Planning could do for people what it already does for floor space. The rules fix floor space by tenure; they could also require reporting on how many tenants return, when, and to the same place or only the same area, and where those who do not return live, with deaths and moves into aged care counted separately. The right to return should say what happens when a tenant dies or enters aged care before the buildings are finished, and the oldest tenants could be moved into the earliest stages so that the wait is shortest for those least able to wait. The share of social housing should be published beside the number of social homes. The permanence of the affordable homes beyond the first 7 per cent, which the applicant says will be held in perpetuity, should be confirmed. Where the existing buildings can be renovated, retention should be considered (Zanardo et al. 2024). None of this assumes bad faith. It asks that the cost of the remedy to the people it is meant to help be counted.

结论一节把前面四组理论放在一起。它们各自带着一个二分：家与房地产，混合与搬迁（隔离与绅士化），弱势聚集与稀释，国家与市场。理论的好处是把复杂的现实分成可以讨论的两端，代价是现实常常不在两端。

案例加深了什么

把 Waterloo 的时间线摊开，每一个二分都变成了有层次、有时间的过程。家与房地产：同一个计划，用一个比例在不同年份里谈判。混合与搬迁：占比降了，套数增了，租户一部分被搬到附近的社会住房，其余的人被承诺回迁。弱势聚集与稀释：规则保证了稀释，但对回迁没有保证。国家与市场：国家既是业主、提案方，也是规划机关，社会住房的资金来自私人住宅的销售。所以，本文的结论“更新在场地上做到了混合，规则也只保证了混合”，实际上是在说：二分是对的，但它们在不同的层次上成立，一个层次上被保证的东西，另一个层次上只是承诺。

汇总

对各理论的汇总

赞同：Stein 和 Davidson 对国家角色的描述；Tammaru 的“不同领域相互关联”，通过 Wynne and Rogers 的“等待已造成伤害”得到支持；Lees 对社会混合证据薄弱的批评，Waterloo 的文件里确实没有结果证据。拓展：Sassen 的全球城市，补上“压力经由地铁站传导”；Rosengren 的第 3 步，补上“被安排的流动”；Atkinson 的置换，分出“周围市场”和“房东直接搬走”两条通道。深化：Madden and Marcuse，冲突发生在国家内部，也发生在对房屋状况的判断里；Shrestha 等的“从外围到核心”，补上药方的来源并非效果证据。限定：Yrjänä 的补贴差距，在 Waterloo 里更像交叉补贴；Aalbers 的金融化，我在这里用得宽松。

局限

这个案例能说和不能说什么

一个案例，只用文件，可以显示机制，不能检验理论。本文的发现只对 2022 年的计划定稿报告、最终规划建议书和设计指南成立；2025 年的评估小组报告说有一项提案要“提高建筑高度、重新分配楼面积，并对设计指南的适用引入灵活性”（NSW Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure 2025a, 1），2026 年 5 至 6 月公示。已读的有公示通知（它列出了修订：降低非住宅楼面积的最低要求、删除第 7.29(4) 条，以及社会住房至少 30% 的概念方案）、开发方的规划说明报告、发出的评估要求，以及 2026 年 4 月环境影响报告的附录 A 至 E。附录里有两处新东西：减缓措施写了“现有社会住房居民在搬迁期间要得到支持”（App. C, PDF p. 11），但没有回迁；设计指南评估把至少 7% 的可负担住房写成永久持有，其余至少 25 年，并预期以后作为同意条件来执行（App. E, PDF p. 6）。所以政策在往前走，只是走在保障的一半：楼面积和年限有了说法，回迁还没有。没有读到提案条文本身、环境影响报告正文和社会影响评估，所以“回迁只是声明”这个结论可能因此改变。这恰好是理论的另一个提醒：政策是持续在变的东西，用一份时点的文件去验证一个理论，容易把一个时点当成了全部。目前没有租户一边的材料，没有回迁和去向的人数，也没有邻里效应的测量。

下一步

可以怎样继续

别处的回迁数字已经有了：Carlton 约 40%、Kensington 20%（Porter et al. 2023, 30），芝加哥不到 11%（Joseph and Chaskin 2012, 378）。下一步最有价值的是三件事：读 2026 年的提案，看回迁是否写进了规则；找 Waterloo 自己的回迁和去向数据；拿 Bonnyrigg 的后续研究做对照，看分阶段的在地搬迁是否真的留住了人。

写作规范

结论里要把“案例显示”“推论”“没有读到”分开写；不使用“证明”“推翻”；贡献写成“小的、基于文件的”，局限写在贡献之后，各有一段。
